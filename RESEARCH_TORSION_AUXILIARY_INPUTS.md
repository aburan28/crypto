# Torsion points and auxiliary inputs as levers on rho and index calculus

**Frozen evidence:** `research/torsion_auxiliary_inputs_20260918/` (harness,
contract in its `README.md`, results in `results/*.json`, tables generated by
`report.py` into `RESULTS.md`).
**Code corrected in the same round:** `src/cryptanalysis/cheon_attack.rs`.
**Scoreboard:** `docs/index-calculus-scoreboard.html`, panel
`auxiliary-inputs-20260918`.

> **Verdict.**  Auxiliary inputs `[α^i]G` are a real lever, torsion points
> are not, and neither touches the plain ECDLP or index calculus.  Cheon's
> `p − 1` algorithm, once actually implemented (the module in this repository
> had been running a plain baby-step/giant-step under Cheon's name), sits on
> its own floor `√(p/d)` at a flat `≈ 20×` with fixed-base tables and beats
> rho from 24 bits up, with a measured exponent of `0.23` against rho's
> `0.50`.  The `p + 1` case sits at `≈ 125–185×` its floor and crosses rho
> near 44 bits at `d ≈ p^{1/3}`.  Both need the protocol to leak `[α^d]G`
> (respectively `[α^{2d}]G`) for a `d` dividing `p ∓ 1`; on the standard
> curves the divisors a realistic leak can reach are tabulated below.  The
> torsion route, an auxiliary elliptic curve's `[d]`-map in place of `F_p^*`,
> is closed by measurement: the only computable comparison costs `p/d`
> pairwise scalar multiplications, `10³–10⁴×` rho at 14–20 bits and rising,
> while an oracle that forms the forbidden quotient in the exponent finds its
> collision in `√(p/d)` samples, so the exponent division is the whole wall.
> Nothing here lowers `Ω(√p)` for an algorithm that sees only `G` and `[α]G`.

## 1. The question, split

"Torsion points and auxiliary inputs for index calculus and Pollard rho
speed-ups" packs three different objects into one phrase.  They have to be
separated before anything can be measured, because they have different
boundaries.

1. **Auxiliary inputs.**  The adversary is handed `G, [α]G, [α²]G, …, [α^q]G`.
   This is the *discrete logarithm with auxiliary inputs* (DLPwAI), the
   problem underlying every `q`-strong-Diffie–Hellman-type assumption
   (Boneh–Boyen signatures, BBG broadcast encryption, traitor tracing, the
   `q`-SDH pairing schemes).  The powers cannot be manufactured from `G` and
   `[α]G` (that is CDH), so this is a statement about protocols that leak
   them, never about a bare ECDLP instance.
2. **Torsion points as an auxiliary group.**  Cheon's algorithm embeds `α`
   into a small subgroup of `F_p^*` (order dividing `p − 1`) or of the
   norm-one torus of `F_{p²}^*` (order dividing `p + 1`).  Kim and Cheon
   asked whether the `δ`-torsion of an auxiliary elliptic curve `E'/F_p`
   with `#E'(F_p) = d·δ` can play the same role, which would free `d` from
   having to divide `p ± 1`.
3. **Torsion points on the target curve.**  Rational points of small order
   on `E` itself.  These are ordinary group elements: they feed
   Pohlig–Hellman on a cofactor, and they act as symmetries of the Semaev
   summation polynomials (Faugère–Gaudry–Huot–Renault 2014; this repository's
   `RESEARCH_SYMMETRIZED_SEMAEV.md` and `RESEARCH_EXOTIC_COORDINATES.md`
   §"prior art").  They do not change the group the logarithm lives in.

The rest of this note measures 1 and 2 and prices 3 by reference to the
existing threads.

## 2. Boundaries, stated before measuring

| boundary | value | derivation |
|:--|:--|:--|
| reference | Pollard rho with negation map on the same subgroup, `S ≈ 1.3` (this round: `0.63–2.0`, median `1.3` over seeds) | `rho.py`, same curves, targets and accounting as every other row |
| floor, plain ECDLP | `Ω(√p)` group operations | Shoup 1997, generic group model; any algorithm seeing only `G, [α]G` |
| floor, DLPwAI with `d` inputs | `Ω(√(p/d))` group operations | Cheon 2010 / Boneh–Boyen generic bilinear model; Cheon–Kim–Song survey §3 |
| unit | `S = total group operations / √p` | affine addition = doubling = 1 op; every phase charged (`ec.Counter`) |

Two ratio columns follow from the two floors: `S / S_rho` and `ops / √(p/d)`.
The auxiliary inputs are given by the problem and are not charged; the
tables say how many are needed, which is the real price.

**Falsification target** (from the contract): a DLPwAI result requires
`ops/√(p/d) ≤ c·log₂p` with `c` flat over ≥ 4 sizes and `S/S_rho` falling
with exponent `−1/4 ± 0.05` (`p − 1`, `d ≈ √p`) or `−1/6 ± 0.05` (`p + 1`,
`d ≈ p^{1/3}`), every seed verified.  A plain-ECDLP result would require a
row that sees only `G, [α]G` to reach `S/S_rho < 0.9`, which the first floor
forbids.  The torsion route is closed if its honest cost is `Θ(p/d)` while
the oracle row collides in `O(√(p/d))`.

## 3. What the literature already settles

- **Cheon 2006 / 2010.**  `d | p − 1`: from `G, [α]G, [α^d]G`, `α` in
  `O(√((p−1)/d) + √d)` exponentiations; `d | p + 1`: from `[α^i]G`,
  `i ≤ 2d`, in `O(√((p+1)/d) + d)` exponentiations.  Kangaroo variants with
  the same cost and `O(Θ·√(p/d))` storage (§3 of the 2010 paper).  Cheon
  notes the result "matches the previously known lower bound of these
  problems in the generic bilinear-group model when `d < p^{1/3}`".
  Brown–Gallant (2004) found the `p − 1` case independently as the static
  Diffie–Hellman attack.
- **Kozaki–Kutsuma–Matsuo 2007.**  Every step is a fixed-base scalar
  multiplication, so precomputed tables turn "exponentiations" into a few
  group operations each.  The comb rows below are this.
- **Izu–Takenaka–Yasuda 2011** (J. Inf. Processing 19): Cheon on a
  pairing-friendly curve over `GF(3^127)`; BSGS and kangaroo cost the same
  (`2^22.0` vs `2^21.3` points per step), kangaroo needs `56 KB` instead of
  `32 MB`.  **Sakemi–Hanaoka–Izu–Takenaka–Yasuda, PKC 2012:** a 160-bit curve
  with an 84-bit prime `d | p − 1`, `≈ 2^38` field operations, `1314` core-days.
- **Satoh 2009; Kim–Cheon–Lee 2014 (Math. Comp.).**  Embedding into `GL_k(F_p)`, i.e.
  `F_{p^k}`, for `d | Φ_k(p)`: for `k ≥ 3` the cost is *not* below square
  root "unless all prime divisors of `D` divide `k` or `p ± 1`" (survey,
  Theorem 4.5 of Kim et al.).  So `p ± 1` is not an accident of the method;
  cyclotomic divisors beyond `k = 2` give nothing.
- **Cheon–Kim 2016** (LMS J. Comput. Math.; ePrint 2012/609).  Replace the
  embedding by a polynomial `f` of degree `d` with a small value set: cost
  `Õ(√(p/τ_f) + d)` with `τ_f` the number of absolutely irreducible factors
  of `f(x) − f(y)`.  `x^d` gives `p − 1`, Dickson polynomials give `p + 1`,
  and "for `d | Φ₃(p)` no polynomial exists that achieves the upper bound".
  Their §5 elliptic embedding is object 2 above, and the survey states the
  obstacle in one line: "computing list `L₂` seems hard".  This note measures
  what that line costs.
- **Shparlinski–Sutherland 2014.**  Deterministic `m·p^{1/2+o(1)}` search
  for `E'/F_p` with `m | #E'(F_p)`: finding the auxiliary curve of object 2
  is not the obstacle either.
- **Faugère–Gaudry–Huot–Renault 2014.**  Rational 2- and 4-torsion (Edwards,
  Jacobi intersections) halves the degree per variable of the summation
  polynomials: a constant-factor Gröbner gain, already reconciled in this
  repository as prior art and classed engineering.

## 4. What was built

`research/torsion_auxiliary_inputs_20260918/`:

- `ec.py` — affine curve arithmetic with an exact operation counter, comb
  tables, Mestre-style prime-order search, curves chosen so that `p − 1`
  (resp. `p + 1`) has a divisor within `p^{0.5±0.06}` (resp. `p^{1/3±0.06}`).
- `rho.py` — the reference: 32-adding walk, negation map, distinguished
  points, cycle escape by doubling.  Each step is one affine addition.
- `cheon.py` — Cheon `p − 1` (BSGS naive, BSGS comb, kangaroo comb) and
  `p + 1` (BSGS comb through `(A(α) + B(α)θ)/C(α)` with the binomial
  expansions of `(x − θ)^{2d}` and `(x² − a)^d`, compared coordinate-wise in
  the exponent), plus `plain_bsgs`, the algorithm the old Rust module ran.
- `torsion_embedding.py` — the Kim–Cheon elliptic route with division
  polynomials `φ_d/ψ_d²` of degree `d²`, in two versions: honest
  (cross-multiplied pairwise test) and oracle (quotient handed over).
- `curve_divisors.py` — the divisors of `n ± 1` a `q`-SDH leak could use
  on secp256k1, P-256, P-384, Curve25519, BN254, BLS12-381 and ECC2K-130.
- `src/cryptanalysis/cheon_attack.rs` — rewritten to implement the algorithm
  its docstring described; the old body is kept as the
  `plain_bsgs_as_in_rust` row.

Every run verifies `[α]G` against the planted secret; a row without that
check is not in the tables.

### What a leak would buy on standard curves

From `results/curve_divisors.md` (trial division to `2^22`, bounded Pollard
rho, plus the verified published factors of secp256k1's `n − 1`; unsplit
cofactors excluded, so each `d` is a lower bound).  `q` is the number of
powers `[α^i]G` the protocol publishes; the usable `d` is the largest
divisor of `n ∓ 1` not exceeding `q`, and the exponent-side gain is `√d`.

Two readings.  The pairing-friendly curves, which are the ones deployed
under `q`-SDH assumptions, have `2^28`–`2^32` dividing `r − 1` by
construction (the FFT-friendly 2-adicity), so a scheme that publishes
`2^32` powers hands over a `2^16` factor.  The plain-ECDLP curves are not
special here in either direction: nobody publishes `[α^i]G` for a
secp256k1 or Curve25519 secret, and if they did the divisors are
comparable.  The `p + 1` side is never better than the `p − 1` side by more
than a few bits on these curves, and it needs twice the inputs.

## 6. Reading it

**The reference behaves.**  Rho's `S` is `0.63–2.0` across 24–48 bits,
median `1.3`, fitted exponent `0.487 ± 0.031` (`p − 1` curves) and
`0.503 ± 0.015` (`p + 1` curves), against `1/2`.  The negation map is in,
so the expected step count is `√(πp)/2 ≈ 0.89√p`; the rest is
distinguished-point overhead, restarts and the fruitless-cycle escapes.

**Cheon `p − 1` sits on its floor.**  With comb tables the cost is
`15–34×` `√(p/d)` at every size from 24 to 48 bits with no trend (the
`44`-bit value is the one with `d = p^{0.54}`, where the `√d` second search
is the larger of the two); without tables it is `86–372×`, which is the
`≈ 1.5·log₂p` cost of a scalar multiplication showing through.  The
falsification target asked for `ops/√(p/d) ≤ c·log₂p` with `c` flat: `c`
is `0.4–0.9` for the comb rows and `3.5–8.5` for the naive rows, over seven
sizes.  Met.

**The exponent is the DLPwAI exponent, not rho's.**  Fitted
`log₂ops / log₂p` is `0.231 ± 0.006` (comb), `0.297 ± 0.008` (naive),
`0.310 ± 0.024` (kangaroo), all against a predicted `1/4` plus a
logarithmic factor; rho fits `0.487`.  So `S/S_rho` falls with exponent
`≈ −0.26`, inside the target's `−1/4 ± 0.05`.  The ratio column reads
`0.28` at 24 bits and `0.004` at 48 bits for the comb variant, i.e. a
`250×` reduction at 48 bits from one extra input.  Extrapolated, not
measured: at `2^256` with `d ≈ 2^128` the comb variant would cost
`≈ 21·2^{64}` operations against rho's `2^{128}`, which is the textbook
Cheon number; no protocol leaks `2^{128}` powers, so the standard-curve
table above is the operative one.

**Kangaroo costs `3–12×` BSGS and needs `Θ`-fraction memory.**  Its
exponent (`0.31`) is the same up to noise, its constant is worse because
each jump is a fresh fixed-base multiplication on both kangaroos and the
cyclic exponent space forces restarts; Izu–Takenaka–Yasuda found the two
converge with tuned distinguished points.  Engineering, memory class.

**Cheon `p + 1` pays for its `2d` inputs up front and crosses rho
later.**  `125–185×` its floor `√(p/d)` at `d ≈ p^{1/3}`, flat over 24–40
bits; fitted exponent `0.355 ± 0.015` against `1/3` plus the log factor.
The polynomial phase (`3(2d+1)` multi-scalar terms) is `85–93%` of the
total at every size: it is the price of the `2d` inputs and it is linear in
`d`, so the `+d` in Cheon's `O(√(p/d) + d)` is not hidden.  `S/S_rho` is
`7.0` at 24 bits, `1.37` at 40 bits, exponent `≈ −0.15` against the
target's `−1/6 ± 0.05`.  Met, and it says that at `d ≈ p^{1/3}` the
`p + 1` route needs `p ≳ 2^{44}` before it pays at all.

**The old Rust module was worse than rho.**  `plain_bsgs_as_in_rust` is
`0.85–2.7×` rho, exactly `d + (p−1)/d` with `d ≈ √p`, and its
`predicted_cost` field quoted `√d + √((n−1)/d)`.  Accounting class; the
module now implements what it claimed.

**The torsion route has two walls, and both are above rho.**  The honest
rows cost `500–12,000×` rho at 14–20 bits and the ratio grows with `p`.
The phase columns in `results/torsion_*.json` say where: building `L₂`
costs `√(p/d)·d²·log₂p` operations (each of the `√δ` entries is a
multi-scalar sum over the `d²` auxiliary inputs), which already exceeds
`√p·log₂p` for every `d ≥ 2`; and the only computable comparison is the
cross-multiplied pairwise test, `p/d` scalar multiplications in the worst
case (two of twelve runs found no matching pair in the `3m × m` table and
paid the full `p/d`, recorded as failures).  The oracle rows, handed the
quotient `φ_d(rα)/ψ_d²(rα)` for free, collide after `6–533` samples at
`33–139×` the floor, i.e. the `√δ` birthday count is real.  The wall is
exactly the survey's one sentence: the quotient cannot be formed in the
exponent.  Closed, by the contract's own criterion.

## 7. Torsion points on the target curve: priced by reference

Three ways a small-order rational point `T ∈ E(F_q)` can enter an ECDLP
attack, none of which moves either floor:

- **Cofactor.**  If `#E = h·p` with `T` of order dividing `h`, Pohlig–Hellman
  reduces to the prime-order subgroup; the `Ω(√p)` floor is on that subgroup
  and rho already runs there.  A point of order `ℓ | p` does not exist unless
  `ℓ = p`.
- **Symmetry for index calculus.**  Translation by `T` and negation generate
  a group of order `2^{m−1}m!` (or `4^{m−1}m!` for 4-torsion) acting on the
  `m` summands of a decomposition; quotienting by it lowers the summation
  polynomial's degree per variable from `2^{m−1}` to `2^{m−2}`.  This is
  FHJRV's subject, reconciled as prior art in `RESEARCH_EXOTIC_COORDINATES.md`
  §"prior art".  It moves the Gröbner constant, not the counting floor
  `κ ≥ √(2(B+1)/γ)`: the automorphism order `γ` grows by the same factor the
  relation yield does, which is why the scoreboard lists it under
  engineering.  The `2-torsion-saturated` factor bases in
  `RESEARCH_KOBLITZ_INDEX_CALCULUS.md` measured `8.9×` *worse* than the
  unsaturated base at equal size, a relabelling.
- **Rank-two torsion.**  `E(F_q) ≅ Z/n₁ × Z/n₂` with `n₁ > 1` puts the full
  `n₁`-torsion over `F_q`; the Weil pairing on `E[n₁]` maps its logarithm to
  `F_q^*`, but that is the MOV reduction for the small part `n₁ | q − 1`, and
  the prime-order part is untouched (this repository's
  `RESEARCH_ECC2K130_DECOMPOSITION_RUNS.md` hit the rank-two case at
  `#E = 23²` and correctly declined to pose the logarithm there).

For **index calculus with auxiliary inputs** the same boundary argument
applies with the roles reversed: the inputs `[α^i]G` are group elements
whose logarithms `α^i` are polynomially related, and the relation matrix
treats logarithms linearly.  Decomposing `[α^i]G` over a factor base yields
a relation with a new unknown `α^i` per input, one relation per unknown, so
the system's rank deficit is unchanged and the polynomial tie `α^i = (α)^i`
is invisible to the linear algebra.  The only known way to *use* the tie is
Cheon's exponent embedding, which is a collision search, not a relation
collector.  No index-calculus row is claimed, and none was measured, for
that reason: there is no candidate algorithm to put in it.

## 9. What this is not

- Not an ECDLP speed-up.  Every row that beats rho was handed `[α^d]G` or
  `[α^{2d}]G`; the reference row could not use them and no row that sees
  only `G, [α]G` moved.
- Not a statement about `d ∤ p ± 1`.  Kim et al. close `Φ_k(p)`, `k ≥ 3`, and
  the torsion route is closed here; the polynomial-value-set route remains
  open in the literature only as "find a degree-`d < p^{1/3}` polynomial
  with value set `< cp/d`", which the survey calls unproved to exist.
- Not an index-calculus result: §7 gives the reason there is no row.
- Not a constant that survives hardware: the `≈ 20×` over the DLPwAI floor
  is the comb-table exponentiation cost at 24–48 bits and grows like
  `log p / w`; the Fujitsu implementations report `log r` factors of the
  same kind.

## 10. Next concrete actions

1. **None on the plain ECDLP** from this direction; the floor is the
   reason, and this round did not find an accounting hole in it.
2. **Protocol audit, not curve audit.**  The usable `d` on a standard curve
   is set by how many powers the protocol leaks (§5 last table).  A `q`-SDH
   deployment on BLS12-381 or BN254 with `q ≥ 2^32` hands over a `2^16×`
   rho reduction on the exponent side; that is a parameter statement for
   pairing-based schemes, and the place to look next is which deployed
   schemes publish `[α^i]G` for `i` in the thousands or beyond.
3. **Kangaroo memory.**  The kangaroo row costs `2–4×` the BSGS row in
   operations and `√(p/d)/Θ` less memory; that trade is where a
   large-instance run would be engineered, and the Fujitsu numbers say the
   two converge with distinguished-point tuning.
