# Pentanomial coordinates for the fused sigma walk

`PACKED_PENTA_STATE=1` (`ECC_PACKED_PENTA_STATE`) keeps the walk's
coordinates in `F_2[t]/(t^131 + t^13 + t^2 + t + 1)`, Certicom's own
polynomial basis for ECC2K-130, instead of the optimal polynomial basis the
packed backend has used since [SINGLE-PRODUCT.md](SINGLE-PRODUCT.md). It
requires the Frobenius-table machinery of [SIGMA-TABLE.md](SIGMA-TABLE.md)
and the fused schedule, and changes nothing about the iteration function,
the distinguished-point rule, reports or checkpoints: the walk is the same
sequence of field elements, held in different coordinates.
`make gpu-rtx-pro6000-penta` builds it.

## Why

`roofline.py` on the table build ([SIGMA-TABLE.md](SIGMA-TABLE.md), "Where
the update's time goes") puts the carry-less unit at 82–87% of its measured
rate, the busiest pipe, with 38.1 `CLMAD`s per scalar update, five more than
the table walk's 33.1: the optimal-polynomial-basis modulus is dense, and
the generated reducer spends one `CLMAD` per product on its quotient
(441 ALU lanes per update on top). [THROUGHPUT-30B.md](THROUGHPUT-30B.md)
lever 1 priced the sparse basis and set it aside because the normal-basis
conversion it needs stops being structured; with the conversion now a
nibble-table lookup, that cost is 170 lanes rather than a gathered L1 read.
In the pentanomial basis the reduction is a four-tap shift fold of about 36
logic operations and no `CLMAD`, so the unit's floor moves from 19.3 to
22.2 B/s at the measured 1.62 lanes per SM-clock, and the ALU loses the
reducer's quotient work.

## Arithmetic

* **Products** are the same carryless 131 x 131-bit product (`product131`,
  six `CLMAD`s plus the top-bit correction) followed by `reducePenta131`:
  the 130 bits above `t^130` fold back through taps 13, 2, 1 and 0, then the
  at most 15 bits the taps pushed past `t^130` fold once more.
* **Squaring** spreads the coefficients and reduces the same way.
* **The weight** needs normal-basis coordinates: map 8 of the shared-memory
  table, `pentanomial -> ONB`, replaces `fromPolynomial131`.
* **The Frobenius steps** `x + sigma^j(x)` are maps 0..7, built like the
  optimal-basis ones from `T (I + sigma^j) T^-1` with the generated change
  of basis in place of the parity recursion.
* **The inverse** goes through the normal basis: map 8 in, the existing
  Itoh–Tsujii chain with its permutation networks, map 9 (`ONB ->
  pentanomial`) out. Two lookups per inversion, amortised over the batch.
* **Restarts, reports and checkpoints** convert through the same two maps
  (the restart kernel reads the global copy; checkpoints stay in the normal
  basis as before).

The change of basis is generated by `codegen/genpenta.py`: a root of the
pentanomial is found in the normal-basis model by Cantor–Zassenhaus
(`field.findRootInOnb` through `curves.basisImages`, the path `gen.py`
already uses for the challenge points), and the two 131 x 131 matrices are
checked to round-trip and to commute with products, squaring and `sigma^7`
before `include/packedpenta131.h` is written; `make check-cli` regenerates
and compares it. The ten maps live in a table of 87,040 bytes (ten 8,704-byte
maps, each 33 nibble positions x 16 rows of 16 bytes plus three parity masks
for bits 128..130, padded to keep every position group 256-byte aligned),
staged into shared memory once per block.

## Validation

* `make test-packed` and `make test-packed-network` check, in every host
  build, `reducePenta131` against long division modulo the pentanomial on
  all 261 basis vectors and 1,000 random products (poisoned high bits
  included), the change of basis on every basis vector, and 400 products,
  squares, inverses, paired products and basis maps against the independent
  reference together with all eight sigma maps (3,200 lookups).
* `src/testpackedcuda.cu`, compiled with the knob, runs every map through
  shared memory on the device (1,320 lookups: basis vectors, the dense
  element and random inputs for each of the ten maps) and the device reducer
  on 1,261 products, against the host.
* The complete-client gates are the same as the table's: replay, restarts,
  byte-identical resume, the overdue guard, 300 host-replayed reports and
  sorted-corpus identity with the table build.

## Measurement: rejected at 0.785

[benchmarks/sigma-table/gpujob-penta.sh](benchmarks/sigma-table/gpujob-penta.sh)
ran the table build with the persisting window at one wave of workers
against the same build in pentanomial coordinates, one RTX PRO 6000 (driver
580.95.05), four rotating rounds at 32 launches after every gate (arithmetic
suites including the device map and reducer probes, client integration,
300-report replay, identical 1,709,477-record corpora):

| build | B/s, four rounds | median |
|---|---|---:|
| table + persist | 16.913, 16.917, 16.918, 16.918 | 16.918 |
| pentanomial | 13.272, 13.273, 13.273, 13.273 | 13.273 |

Ratio **0.784571**, every round within 0.01%; 128 registers, 48 bytes of
local memory, the GPU at its maximum clock with 100 W to spare. Receipts
under [benchmarks/sigma-table/penta/](benchmarks/sigma-table/penta/).

## Reading

The arithmetic saved what it was meant to save; what it added was one more
table lookup on every slot, and the lookups are the walk's real bottleneck.
A 131-bit nibble lookup moves 33 rows of 16 bytes plus 64 bytes of masks, about
600 bytes of shared memory per lane. The table build's two Frobenius lookups
per update are therefore about 1.2 KB per update, which at 16.9 B/s is 45 bytes
per SM-clock against the shared-memory pipe's measured 64 bytes per SM-clock
(THROUGHPUT-CEILING.md): roughly 70% busy, a figure `roofline.py` does not show
because it prices a 128-bit load as one lane. This is also why
`SIGMA_TAG_DENOM` lost 20% (one extra lookup in the reverse pass) and why the
table itself netted only +1.8% over the one-block geometry despite removing
18% of the ALU work. In the pentanomial basis the weight's normal-basis view
costs a third lookup per slot, which saturates the pipe, and no CLMAD or ALU
saving can buy that back.

What would change the verdict is a lookup that moves fewer bytes: byte-indexed
rows halve the traffic but need 70 KB per map, five-bit chunks save 16% at
14 KB per map, and eight maps have to fit beside each other in 99 KB. The
structured `fromPolynomial131` of the optimal polynomial basis is what makes
that basis the right one for the weight, which is why the table build keeps it.
The knob stays for the record; its arithmetic and tables are tested in every
host build.
