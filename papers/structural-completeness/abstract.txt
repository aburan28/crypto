Title:
  Structural Completeness of Prime-Field Elliptic Curves Against
  Isogeny-Graph Cryptanalysis

Keywords:
  elliptic curve cryptography, ECDLP, isogeny graphs, complex
  multiplication, Howe gluing, Jacobian covers, secp256k1, P-256,
  structural-completeness theorem

Abstract:

We pursue a comprehensive cryptanalytic audit of the isogeny-graph
structure of deployed prime-field elliptic curves --- specifically
secp256k1, NIST P-256, brainpoolP256r1, and Curve25519's prime
subgroup. For each curve we run a battery of structural tests and
two novel attack proposals (Class-Group-Amortised Hidden-Number
Cryptanalysis; Volcano-Floor Class-Group-Augmented Pollard rho),
finding all directions structurally null at the prime-field level.

The main result is a structural-completeness theorem: for every
ordinary elliptic curve E over a prime field F_p with #E(F_p) = h*r,
r prime and h in {1,2,4,8}, no algorithm in the family
{F_p-isogeny transport, Cl(End)-orbit walking, (N,N)-cover via
Howe-gluing, higher-genus cover, supersingular-reduction attack,
volcano-floor rho} reduces ECDLP cost below sqrt(r / |Aut(E)|)
(plain Pollard rho with full automorphism folding). The theorem
says nothing about algorithms outside this family.

The theorem is argued by case analysis over seven structural blocks
(proof sketches in Section 8, not yet independently reviewed) under
standing assumptions: Tate's isogeny theorem, Howe's gluing
theorem, Gaudry's and Diem-Thome's complexity bounds for
hyperelliptic index calculus, and the assumption that prime-field
hyperelliptic DLP is not sub-exponentially solvable. The empirical
checks are bounded: the structural conditions are computed for the
four deployed curves only; the twist l-rank lemma is checked
numerically for l in {3,5,7,11,13} on j = 0 curves over F_p with
p < 500; the split-Jacobian toy sweep covers primes p = 1 (mod 6)
with p <= 1000, and the CM-73 sweep those with p <= 5000. A
surprising auxiliary finding: the Howe-gluing genus-2 cover exists
for every such curve, but does not yield a DLP speedup because
genus-2 prime-field DLP costs O(p) while ECDLP costs O(sqrt(p)).
For secp256k1 specifically, x^3 + 7 is irreducible over F_p, so the
cover's field of definition is F_{p^3} and the cover-based attack
costs O(p^{3/2}), about 2^384.

A companion documentation note covers Mestre's algorithm for the
actual explicit construction of the Howe-glued cover (open
implementation task) and a parallel research proposal launches the
orthogonal GLV-aware Hidden-Number Problem direction for the
scalar-side attack surface.

Companion artefacts (all open source, in the same repository as
this submission):

  - 13 PARI/GP scripts (~3000 lines) with committed baseline
    outputs for reproducibility
  - 1 Rust integration test (~390 lines) running the full audit on
    every CurveParams constant via cargo test, plus a
    freshly-generated CM curve at test time
  - 5 supporting research notes covering the individual proposals
    and the GLV-HNP / Mestre directions
