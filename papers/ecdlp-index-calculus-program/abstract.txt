Index calculus is the method that should exploit the most structure in an elliptic-curve discrete-logarithm instance, and in practice it has never beaten Pollard rho on a prime-order curve of cryptographic shape. This paper surveys where that question stands and measures it. We review the literature by field type - extension fields (Gaudry, Diem, FGHR), prime-degree binary fields and the contested first-fall-degree heuristics behind the subexponential claims (Petit-Quisquater, Semaev, and the evidence of Huang, Kosters and Yeo, Shantz-Teske and Galbraith-Gebregiyorgis), subfield curves (Galbraith-Granger-Merz-Petit) and prime fields (Petit-Kosters-Messeng, Amadori-Pintore-Sala, McGuire-Mueller) - and the generic lower bounds that frame them. We then measure the frontier with one native harness that charges rho, baby-step giant-step, the kangaroo and several index-calculus pipelines in a single counted unit on one-target workloads with sealed, replayable records. Almost no cost in such records is complete, the rho baselines included, so we price every arm's unpriced native work alike across a band of weights and rank two methods only where the order holds across the band. On fixed ladders of eight Koblitz curves (log2 r from 11 to 39), six NIST-shaped prime curves (20-40 bits) and five Kummer-tower curves - 4,848 executions on public targets, parameters chosen on separate training targets, every session replay-audited - no index-calculus arm beats generic rho on any curve at any weight. The closest, a compact Frobenius-orbit factor base, is 3.8-15x signed-Frobenius rho with native work free; two-summand prime-field index calculus goes from 10.5x to 102x rho between 20 and 40 bits; no fitted cost exponent of a campaign index-calculus arm lies below rho's. The measurements come from a fourteen-week engineering programme that we also document: root-finding decomposition oracles (a known mechanism; its root identity, Proposition 1, is proved in Lean, and the oracle is engineered to 383-1,535x over matrix F4 per decomposition), an F_p F4 brought to msolve parity on square systems, FGHR's D_4 symmetry cutting the k=4 solve over F_{p^4} by 48x, genus-three index calculus after the cubic GHS transfer at about q^1.4 against rho's q^1.5 (the only exponent below rho's; its distance to a measured negation-map rho at q=2^20 fell from 2.6x to 1.15x in three improvement rounds), and the negative results, including a conservation lemma for isogeny transfers and an equal-advice audit that retires the programme's online win over rho: Bernstein-Lange preprocessing rho with the same table is 53-317x faster. We close with open problems, each stated as the experiment or proof that would settle it. Nothing here concerns a deployed curve or key recovery.
