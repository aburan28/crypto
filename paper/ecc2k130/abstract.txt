ECC2K-130 is the Certicom Koblitz challenge $y^2+xy=x^3+1$ over
$\FF_{2^{131}}$. It has been open since 1997, and the best published
estimate puts it at $2^{60.9}$ iterations of a Frobenius-class Pollard
rho. We report a campaign against it on hardware you can rent today,
with every rate measured against a paired control. The field is built
on a type-II optimal normal basis from a single generated golden model.
On one RTX~PRO~6000, sixteen paired optimisation steps take a packed
CUDA client from a $0.85$ billion-iterations-per-second bitsliced
control to $14.64$, the build the campaign ships; later builds of the
same walk reach $15.88$, and a table walk reaches $20.08$, though only
as a rate, not yet as a cost per solve. A VHDL engine runs at $8.25$~G
steps/s on one AWS F2 FPGA, inside its pre-board estimate of $5$--$12$.
We measure the walk constant instead of assuming it: the campaign's
walk needs $2^{60.91}$--$2^{60.92}$ iterations, so the 2009 budget holds.
A floor derived from the GPU's carry-less unit before any tuning rules
out $28$~B/s on one card. We then apply the same optimisations to
Pollard rho on NIST P-256 and P-384 on the same GPU. Explicit carry
chains, a wider shared inversion and the same refusal of register-cap
trades take the walk from $1.075$ to $1.632$~G steps/s on P-256
($1.52\times$) and from $0.210$ to $0.548$ on P-384 ($2.61\times$); the
levers transfer, but their relative sizes do not. The campaign, at one
distinguished point per $2^{28.41}$ iterations, has stored $486$ million
points and $2^{56.68}$ iterations, $5.4\%$ of the expected work, without
a collision; its fleet was reclaimed on 2026-09-18 and collection has
been paused since 2026-10-07. Index calculus on the curve, priced in the
same unit, comes out $10^7$ times more expensive than rho, and the best
measured point decomposition is $35$--$51$ bits short of its budget.
Every number is footnoted to a file in a public repository. None of
this improves on the generic-group bound, and we do not claim that it
does.
