PART G — root choice: is it a free attacker degree of freedom?  NO.
==============================================================================
A j=0 curve has two GLV eigenvalues lam and lam' = n-1-lam = lam^-1.
Both give a valid HNP instance, so an attacker may use whichever has
the smaller nu_hat.  HYPOTHESIS (this run's own): the two roots are
untied, so min over roots is a free ~2x shot at a low-nu_hat instance.
RESULT BELOW: FALSIFIED -- the gain is exactly 1.00x.

Reversal theorem check (CF(n/lam) reversed vs CF(n/(+/-lam^-1))):
  reversed == CF(n/ +lam^-1) : 0/200   <- the OTHER GLV root
  reversed == CF(n/ -lam^-1) : 103/200   <- not a GLV root
  So the REVERSAL theorem does not tie the two GLV roots: it maps
  lam to -lam^-1 (parity-dependent), and the second GLV root is
  +lam^-1.  A different, elementary relation ties them instead:

    lam'/n = 1 - lam/n - 1/n,  and  x = [0;1,a2,a3,...]
                                =>  1-x = [0;1+a2,a3,...]
  i.e. the two expansions share a TAIL after a 1-term realignment,
  perturbed only by the -1/n.  Aligned-prefix agreement measured:

  secp256k1: 68 of 142 partial quotients agree after alignment (48%),
             and the winning index j* ~ 70 sits just past that point.
  24-bit curves: agreement fraction q10=0.22 q50=0.31 q90=0.36
  CAVEAT (open): at 24 bits only ~1/3 of the expansion is shared, yet
  |d nu| is still ~0.012 at eff=0.05.  Prefix sharing alone does not
  fully account for how tightly the two roots track; not resolved here.
