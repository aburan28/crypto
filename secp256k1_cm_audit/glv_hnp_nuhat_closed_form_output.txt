  *** RESULT: GLV eigenvalues DO NOT follow the random-lattice law. ***
  Uniform lam matches 3v^2/pi (as Part D shows); GLV lam is shifted UP.
  But the shift is SCALE-DEPENDENT, and that detail matters -- read the
  decay table below before quoting any single number.

    At eff=0.10, P(nu_hat <= 0.645) (the recorded C2 ceiling):
      GLV lam  39.3%   uniform lam  39.8%   closed form  39.7%
    -- i.e. at THIS cut and THIS scale the GLV constraint has already
       washed out; the KS deviation above lives in the extreme low tail
       (q05: GLV 0.331 vs closed form 0.229).

  Mechanism.  For a j=0 CM curve, lam is the eigenvalue of an
  endomorphism of norm 1, so Lambda = <(n,0),(-lam,1)> is the coordinate
  lattice of the norm-n ideal (n, omega - lam) in Z[omega].  Its shortest
  vector is pinned near sqrt(n) by the Eisenstein norm form
  a^2+ab+b^2 = n -- that pinning is precisely what makes GLV scalar
  decomposition work.  So the very structure that makes a curve
  GLV-friendly also holds nu_hat up, away from the low-nu_hat tail.
  (It is not pinned exactly to the Hermite corner because L2 uses the
  coordinate metric, not the Minkowski metric of Z[omega].)

  DECAY OF THE GLV CONSTRAINT with the scale ratio S_K1/S_K2 ~ 1/eff.
  (K1=K2 is eff ~ 1; the Phase-2 runs used eff = 0.05-0.50.)

    eff     P(nu<=0.645) GLV   uniform     median GLV   median unif
    1.00        0.0%           42.2%       0.8770       0.7166
    0.75        0.0%           42.0%       0.8494       0.7183
    0.50        0.0%           39.2%       0.7709       0.7288
    0.25       39.5%           37.0%       0.7382       0.7306
    0.10       44.2%           40.0%       0.6947       0.7101
    0.05       43.8%           41.8%       0.6935       0.6953
    0.01       39.8%           42.2%       0.7242       0.6991

  Reading: the GLV/Eisenstein constraint is decisive only near eff ~ 1
  (S_K1 ~ S_K2), where L2 is essentially the ideal lattice itself.  As
  eff falls the weighting skews L2 away from that ideal and the
  constraint washes out; by eff = 0.10 GLV and uniform agree at the
  0.645 cut to well under a percentage point.  So 'GLV curves are
  protected' is TRUE at eff ~ 1 and FALSE in the eff <= 0.10 regime the
  Phase-2 experiments actually ran in.  Quote the eff.
