EXP E: the gap law as a falsifiable prediction — m-scaling at fixed eff
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Closed form for the quotient lattice (S_K1=n/K1, S_K2=n/K2, S_KAN=n):
    det L' = n^(2m) / eff^m,   dim = 2m+1,   ||v'|| = n*sqrt(2m/3+1)
    gap(m, eff, n) = sqrt((2m+1)/(2*pi*e*(2m/3+1)))
                     * n^(-1/(2m+1)) * eff^(-m/(2m+1))
As m -> infinity this tends to eff^(-1/2)*sqrt(3/(2*pi*e)), so gap >= 1
iff eff <= 3/(2*pi*e) = 0.1756 -- a curve-INDEPENDENT ceiling strictly
below the information-theoretic bound eff <= n^(-1/m) -> 1.

2026-07-29 T4b tested m = 8,12,16,24,32 at K1=8 (eff=0.157) on the
lam*=0.07 curve, all failed, and concluded 'the K1 wall is genuine, not
sample size'.  The gap law says gap(32) = 0.910 < 1 so those m were ALL
too small, and predicts the crossing near m ~ 100.  Testing that now.

EXP F: why the Gaussian heuristic does not apply — the m-independent
       lambda-block parasite
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The quotient lattice contains m disjoint copies of the 2-D sublattice
    L2 = < (n*S_K1, 0), (-lam*S_K1, S_K2) >   (columns k1_i, k2_i)
whose exact shortest vector mu = lambda_1(L2) is computed in O(log n) by
Lagrange-Gauss (`lambda_block_mu`, glv_hnp_phase2_lambda_threshold.py:209)
and does NOT depend on m.  If the observed shortest vector equals mu then
lambda_1(L') is pinned at an m-independent value while ||v'|| grows like
sqrt(m), so the Gaussian heuristic over-estimates lambda_1 and the gap law
of EXP E must fail.  mu is the same quantity that gives the ratio
nu_hat = mu/sqrt(det L2), the 2026-07-29 separator (AUC 0.935).
