# Research Note: Isogeny-Loop Endomorphisms for GLV on Koblitz, Binary and Prime Curves

**Date.** 2026-10-10.  **Status.** Tool landed and validated; sweeps measured on public
curves.  **Question addressed.** Whether loops of horizontal isogenies on the crater of
the endomorphism ring (Koshelev–Sanso, ePrint 2024/1985) give cheap non-scalar
endomorphisms, and hence GLV/GLS-type scalar-multiplication speed-ups, on the curves
this repository works on: the Koblitz curves ECC2K-130, K_0 / K_1 over GF(2^83),
ECC2K-95, ECC2K-108; the random binary and prime Certicom curves ECC2-131, ECCp-131,
ECC2-109, ECCp-109, ECC2-97, ECCp-97, ECC2-89, ECCp-89, ECC2-79, ECCp-79; sect131r1,
sect131r2, sect113r1; and, as controls, secp256k1 and the GOST CryptoPro-B curve
(D = −619) whose isogeny-chain GLV this repository already measured
(`research/cryptopro_b_glv_chain_20261005`).
**Code.** `scripts/isogeny_loop_sweeper.py` (Python, cypari2 / PARI 2.17).
**Evidence.** `research/isogeny_loop_glv_20261010/` (one Markdown table and one JSON
per sweep group, the self-test transcript, the scans).

---

## 1. The construction (what the paper does, in the terms used here)

Let E/F_q be ordinary with Frobenius trace t, Δ = t² − 4q = f_π² · D_K with D_K the
fundamental discriminant of K = Q(√Δ), and End(E) = O an order of discriminant
D = g² D_K with g | f_π.  The crater of every ℓ-isogeny volcano of E is acted on freely
and transitively by the class group Cl(O): the prime ideals 𝔩, 𝔩̄ above a split prime ℓ
(ℓ ∤ g, (D_K/ℓ) = 1) are the two horizontal ℓ-isogenies out of every crater curve.

A closed non-backtracking walk of horizontal isogenies E → E_1 → … → E_m = E of
prime degrees ℓ_1, …, ℓ_m is an endomorphism φ of E of degree ℓ_1⋯ℓ_m.  Evaluating it
costs about Σ c(ℓ_i) field multiplications (one precomputed Vélu/Kohel step each),
not c(ℓ_1⋯ℓ_m).  The walks that close are exactly the vectors of the **relation
lattice**

    Λ = { e ∈ Z^k : ∏_i [𝔩_i]^{e_i} = 1 in Cl(O) }

(e_i > 0: e_i steps along 𝔩_i, e_i < 0: |e_i| steps along 𝔩̄_i), and the endomorphism is
a generator α of the principal ideal ∏ 𝔩_i^{e_i}, with N(α) = ∏ ℓ_i^{|e_i|}.  The
cheapest loop is the shortest nonzero vector of Λ in the **weighted 1-norm**
Σ c(ℓ_i)|e_i| (paper, Section 4; Section 3 is the special case of a single prime,
where the loop length is the order of [𝔩] in Cl(O)).  On E(F_q)[r], α acts as
λ = α mod (r, π − 1); the GLV split k = k_1 + k_2 λ then replaces ℓ' = ⌈log₂ r / 2⌉
doublings by one evaluation of α.  The paper's benchmark is therefore
cost(α) against 8ℓ' multiplications (c = 7.5ℓ per ℓ-step, doubling = 8 M, both in
projective coordinates); a loop is worthwhile iff cost(α) ≪ 8ℓ'.

## 2. The sweeper

`scripts/isogeny_loop_sweeper.py` turns this into a search tool:

1. **Frobenius order.** Δ = t² − 4q is factored (trial division to 2²⁰, then `factorint`
   under a time budget), D_K and f_π are split off.  The maximal order O_K is analysed
   by default: Cl(O_g) → Cl(O_K) is surjective, so every loop in a sub-order is a loop
   in O_K and the O_K result is a lower bound on the cost for any End(E).
   `--conductor g` pins End(E) = O_g; the O_g relation sub-lattice is computed from the
   O_K lattice through `idealstar`/`ideallog` (α ∈ O_g up to units ⇔ its image in
   (O_K/g)^* lies in the subgroup generated by (Z/g)^* and O_K^*).
2. **Class group and dlogs.** `bnfinit` (GRH) on x² − D_K; for every prime ℓ ≤ L
   (default 100) that splits, `idealprimedec` + `bnfisprincipal` give the class-group
   discrete log of one prime above ℓ, the order of its class, and `nfmodpr` gives the
   **Frobenius eigenvalue π mod 𝔩 ∈ F_ℓ**, which is what selects the kernel E[𝔩] =
   {Q ∈ E[ℓ] : π(Q) = [π mod 𝔩]Q} in an implementation.
3. **Relation lattice.** Λ is the projection of the integer kernel of [dlogs | diag(cyc)]
   (`matkerint`, `mathnf`).
4. **Cheapest loops.** LLL on the weighted Gram matrix Bᵀ W B gives an upper bound
   (the paper's Q_w approximation); then an exact depth-first enumeration of the
   weighted-1-norm ball {v ∈ Z^k : Σ c_i |v_i| ≤ R}, R = the GLV saving 8ℓ' by default,
   with membership v ∈ Λ tested through the dlogs.  The 1-norm ball is tiny compared
   with the Q_w ellipsoid that Lemma 1 of the paper would require (for CryptoPro-B the
   ellipsoid holds ≈ 10¹⁰ lattice points, the 1-norm ball 7650), so the list of **every**
   loop that beats the doublings it saves is exhaustive whenever the node cap is not
   hit (reported per curve).
5. **Per loop.** α = (A + B√D_K)/2 (via `bnfisprincipal` with generator), N(α) checked
   against ∏ℓ^{|e|}, λ and λ̄ on E(F_q)[r] (√D_K mod r is fixed by π ≡ 1), the
   Gauss-reduced GLV basis and its max |k_i| in bits, whether α ∈ Z[π] (then α = c + dπ
   acts on all of E(F_q) as the integer c + d, which is reported; when f_π = 1 this is
   vacuous because Z[π] = O_K), whether the loop is a Frobenius/Verschiebung power
   π^m (binary curves: the loop {2: m·n}), whether λ = ±1, and the cheapest loop that
   is not a Frobenius power (from the exhaustive list, else as an LLL upper bound).
6. **Above `--max-disc-bits` (140)** the class group is not computed and a Gaussian-
   heuristic estimate of the shortest relation is printed instead (det Λ = h ≈ √|D|/π).
7. **`selftest`** builds a crater curve for D = −71 (h = 7) over a 31-bit prime, walks
   the loops found by the lattice search with PARI's Vélu isogenies, and checks on a
   point of prime order r that the composite is exactly [±λ].  Two things learned while
   making it pass, both relevant to any implementation: (a) for ℓ | f_π (always the
   case for ℓ = 2 in odd characteristic, since horizontal 2-isogenies need t even)
   Frobenius acts as the same scalar on both kernels, so the direction 𝔩 vs 𝔩̄ cannot be
   read from π — the self-test orients those steps by labelling the crater as the Cayley
   graph of Cl(O) from the unambiguous primes; (b) the sign of the composite (±α) is the
   sign of the final isomorphism u, so λ is determined up to sign and is fixed with one
   test point.
8. **`scan`** ranks a range of fundamental discriminants by cheapest loop (for choosing
   D before the CM method; the enumeration radius is the LLL bound, which certifies the
   minimum); **`disc`** analyses one discriminant.  Both list prime-norm loops only, so
   the degree-1 automorphisms of D = −3, −4 do not appear (they beat every loop).

Commands used for every number below are in `research/isogeny_loop_glv_20261010/README.md`.

## 4. Results

Cost model throughout: 7.5ℓ M per ℓ-isogeny step in projective coordinates (paper,
Section 2.2), 2 M per step for ℓ = 2 in characteristic 2 (Frobenius/Verschiebung),
saving = 8ℓ' M.  "Ratio" = cost(α)/saving; below 1 means the loop costs less than the
doublings it removes.

### 4.1 Koblitz curves: the loop is the Frobenius, nothing beyond τ-NAF

| curve | n | D_K | h | cheapest loop | α | cost | ratio |
|:--|--:|--:|--:|:--|:--|--:|--:|
| ECC2K-130 | 131 | −7 | 1 | {2: 1} | (1 − √−7)/2 = τ̄ (or τ) | 2 M | 0.004 |
| icv1-f2m83-tm6151469093347-debefd74 (Koblitz a = 0 over GF(2^83)) | 83 | −7 | 1 | {2: 1} | τ | 2 M | 0.006 |
| icv1-f2m83-t6151469093347-cdcc5432 (Koblitz a = 1 over GF(2^83)) | 83 | −7 | 1 | {2: 1} | τ | 2 M | 0.009 |
| ECC2K-95 | 97 | −7 | 1 | {2: 1} | τ | 2 M | 0.005 |
| ECC2K-108 | 109 | −7 | 1 | {2: 1} | τ | 2 M | 0.005 |

For every binary Koblitz curve Δ = −7·u_n² (the repository's volcano audit
`research/volcano_n53_n83_20260924` already proves D_K = −7), End(E) = Z[τ] = O_K has
class number 1, so **every** prime ideal is principal and every horizontal loop has
length 1: the loop through 𝔩 | ℓ *is* an element a + bτ of Z[τ] of norm ℓ (ℓ = 11:
3 + 2τ; 23: 1 + ... all listed in `group_a_koblitz_and_known.json`).  The cheapest is τ
itself (norm 2, two squarings).  The relation lattice is Z^k, the exhaustive list of
loops under budget is just the powers and products of τ, and everything it finds is
already captured by the τ-adic expansion (τ-NAF, width-w τ-NAF).  **There is no
isogeny-loop endomorphism on ECC2K-130 or on the n = 83 instances beyond what τ-NAF
already uses.**  The sweeper also reports λ_τ and its order mod r (= n), which is the
⟨τ⟩ × ⟨−1⟩ orbit of size 2n that the ECC2K-130 rho walk already exploits.

The same holds for secp256k1 (D = −3, h = 1): the cheapest *non-automorphism* loop is
the degree-7 element (1 + 3√−3)/2 at 52.5 M, which is moot because the automorphism
ω (1 M) is in the same ring.

### 4.2 Why loops cannot help Pollard rho on ECC2K-130

Rho's equivalence-class speed-up needs a *finite* group of efficiently computable
maps with efficiently computable canonical orbit representatives (⟨−1, τ⟩, orbits of
size 2n).  A loop endomorphism α acts on ⟨P⟩ as λ_α of multiplicative order ≈ r, so
its orbits are the whole group; it cannot shrink the search space, and a chain of
isogenies is not cheaper than a point addition anyway.  Loops are a scalar-
multiplication tool (GLV), not a DLP tool; the only places the ECC2K campaign does
scalar multiplications (walk start points, distinguished-point verification) are
already τ-adic.

In characteristic 2 the only 2-isogenies are the Frobenius F (inseparable) and the
Verschiebung V, so the 2-loops are {2: +n} = π (acts as 1 on E(F_q): useless) and
{2: −n} = π̄ = V^n (acts as q ≡ t − 1 (mod r) on E(F_q)).  Both lie in Z[π].  A GLV
split with λ = t − 1 is the paper's "folklore" split: it trades the n/2 doublings of
[t − 1]P for n Verschiebung steps x ↦ x + b_i/x (x-only: 1 M + 2 S per step, but y has
to be recovered).  Under the 2 M/step model the ratio is 0.49; under realistic
binary-field costs (LD doubling ≈ 4 M + 5 S, full-coordinate V step 3–8 M, or two x-only
chains plus a y-recovery) it is close to 1.  **Not established as a gain**; it is not an
isogeny-loop endomorphism in the paper's sense (α ∈ Z[π]).  No loop with α ∉ Z[π]
exists under the budget on any of these curves, exhaustively, and the cheapest
relation the lattice offers is 10²–10⁴ × the saving.

The class number grows like √|D| ≈ √q, the relation lattice has determinant h, and
with k primes the shortest relation has ≈ h^{1/k} entries per coordinate: for a random
131-bit curve, h ≈ 2^65 gives relations of total length ≈ 10²–10³ steps at primes up to
100.  This is the regime the paper calls infeasible ("moderate D" means |D| ≲ 10⁸);
the measurements above quantify it on the actual challenge curves.

## 6. Interpretation (labelled)

* On the Koblitz curves the answer is a clean negative with a proof: Cl(Z[τ]) is
  trivial, so the isogeny graph has no loop that is not already an element of Z[τ].
  The paper's technique is exactly the generalisation of τ-adic methods to rings where
  the cheap element does not exist; on Koblitz curves it does (τ), and τ-NAF is the
  optimum of this family.
* On the random challenge curves the technique is quantified as unusable (ratios
  20–50 at 79–89 bits and far worse at 131 bits) because h ≈ √q.
* The tool is useful for the opposite direction: picking a CM discriminant with a cheap
  loop (scan mode) before constructing a curve, which is where the paper and the
  repository's CryptoPro-B measurement show real gains (1.25–1.34× measured).
