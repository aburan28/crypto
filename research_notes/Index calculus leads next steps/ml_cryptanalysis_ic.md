## Q1. What are csd and csd.lab? Which models, datasets, tasks and results exist (with numbers)?

### Takeaway
`csd` ("Curve Structural Discovery") is a Python/PyTorch learner that asks whether ML can find a structure that makes ECDLP measurably easier; it has run nine milestones of exact-labelled experiments, and the only recoveries are planted or known laws (sqrt(|Aut|/2), volcano structure, Legendre-symbol laws, Humbert surfaces) while every blind cost label (rho cost, IC relation yield, Groebner degrees) came out class-constant or null. `csd.lab` is a config-driven, sharded (local/S3) generator + feature + model + null-control framework that already has an `fpn_degrees` generator for summation-polynomial point decomposition and a ready but unrun `fpn_decomposable` config.

### Cited Findings
**Purpose and evidence levels**
- CSD's question: "can a learning system identify a mathematical transformation that makes a hard computational problem measurably easier, without being taught the transformation?" — [README.md](file:///home/user/ml-cryptanalysis/README.md)
- Three evidence levels: 1 = correlations (held-out on unseen isogeny classes/fields, beating a size-only baseline, with a label-permutation null and a structure-blind control label); 2 = exact certificate; 3 = an algorithm with lower end-to-end measured cost in a frozen paired comparison. "A level-1 signal is a lead, never a result." — [docs/PLAN.md](file:///home/user/ml-cryptanalysis/docs/PLAN.md)
- "Nothing in this repository is an ECDLP speedup claim"; level-3 claims must go through a frozen paired ecbench challenge in the sibling `crypto` repo. Cost unit = one group addition/doubling, matching ecbench. — [README.md](file:///home/user/ml-cryptanalysis/README.md)
- Repository rules: every learned rule cites experiment dir, seed, split and leakage controls; costs are counted group ops, never wall-clock; negative results are kept, never rewritten. — [AGENTS.md](file:///home/user/ml-cryptanalysis/AGENTS.md)
- Relation to sibling program: CSD is the learned-search arm of `crypto-autoresearcher` questions `RQ-RELN-61638a` (GNN, RL policy and symbolic regression on the point-relation hypergraph, with coordinates-only features, leak audit, positive control, permutation null) and `GOAL-ENDO-001`. — [docs/PLAN.md](file:///home/user/ml-cryptanalysis/docs/PLAN.md)

## Q3. What negative results exist (e.g. inability to learn the discrete log), and do they constrain ML-for-IC?

### Takeaway
The repo has no experiment that tries to learn the discrete logarithm (or bits of it) directly; its negative results are about cost/yield/degree labels being class-constant, black-box models on raw coordinates/digits failing on even simple Legendre-symbol laws, and GNNs never beating boosting. These constrain ML-for-IC ideas that target curve-level hardness, but leave per-target, per-instance and per-factor-base decision problems open.

### Cited Findings
- Rho cost labels: "skill over the ceiling-only baseline is within its interval of zero on every split, raw and within class". — [docs/PLAN.md](file:///home/user/ml-cryptanalysis/docs/PLAN.md)
- Typed-edge GNN "never beats boosting on the same features, and its structural-only version stays at zero, as the Cayley-graph argument predicts." — [docs/PLAN.md](file:///home/user/ml-cryptanalysis/docs/PLAN.md)

Kummer-theory limit: no polynomial character in (a, b) can express the 0-vs-3-roots split, needing a feature in an extension (`cardano`). — [docs/LAB.md](file:///home/user/ml-cryptanalysis/docs/LAB.md)
- Yield conserved / degree functionals class-constant (KN-FIND-007 etc.). — [docs/PLAN.md](file:///home/user/ml-cryptanalysis/docs/PLAN.md)

- Grep for "learn the discrete log", "bits of the log", "scalar k" across .py/.md found nothing beyond unrelated hits. — search over [/home/user/ml-cryptanalysis](file:///home/user/ml-cryptanalysis/)

### Inferences
- Constraint 1: predicting which *curves* are IC-weak from isogeny/endomorphism/graph features is already a measured null for prime fields and F_{p^n} at toy sizes; repeating it is low-value.
- Constraint 2: decomposability of a random point is (by the product law) essentially a Bernoulli(gamma) event; a learned decomposability predictor from cheap target features (coordinates, characters of norms) is therefore expected to sit at the base rate unless an algebraic feature (e.g. character of a resultant or discriminant of the descended system) carries the information — the framework's own lesson is that the invariant must be in the feature library.
- Constraint 3: the digit-token transformer failure on Legendre symbols suggests that any "learn the DL" or "learn smoothness from bits" attempt with sequence models is extremely unlikely to work; the repo itself never tested direct DL learning, so this is an absence, not a result.
- Not constrained: per-instance decisions inside a solve — early rejection of spurious E-system solutions, predicting deg_I degeneracy, choice of factor-base model (the subfield-model yield changes are exact and large), ordering/selection of targets, and Groebner-step cost (log_red, log_us) for binary IC.

### Gaps
- No repo evidence on learning DL bits, smoothness of random integers/polys, or learned sieving; no RL policy result for the point-relation hypergraph (RQ-RELN-61638a is referenced but no experiment here).

### Inferences (proposed experiments; none has been run)
1. **Run `fpn_decomposable` as-is** (n = 2, p in 31..251, ~1,000 curves x 8 targets in < 10 CPU-min). Expected: raw/symbol features near base rate (~0.4-0.45 positive), `solutions` feature set ~1.0 (positive control). This establishes the "decomposability is not predictable from cheap target data" negative cleanly with the lab's nulls — a citable constraint for ML-for-IC.
2. **Spurious-solution filter (E system)**: label `solvable_not_decomposable` (22.8%/10.7% at n=2, ~44-65% at n=3). Features: characters of the discriminant of the e-polynomial T^n - e_1 T^{n-1} + ..., of norms of x(R), and of S_{n+1} partial evaluations; run `symdisc` + gbdt. Note: n_split (whether the e-polynomial splits over F_p) is the exact decider, so the real question is whether a cheaper-than-root-finding predictor exists; at n = 2 the discriminant character decides splitting exactly, so the interesting case is n = 3 (cubic splitting: the `cardano`-type feature is the known decider). Expectation: a rediscovery of known Galois-theory laws (good positive control), not a speedup.
3. **n = 3 degeneracy (deg_I < Bezout, rate ~1/p)**: the report frames it as a codimension-one condition on the target; a nullspace learner like the Humbert one (monomials in x(R) coordinates, nullspace mod p) could recover the hypersurface equation exactly — a level-2 result, cheap (needs more p = 13..31 targets with forced degeneracy).
4. **Factor-base model choice**: yield is a model label (subfield model: gamma exactly 1, 1/2, 1/4 at n = 2; collapse to E_0(F_p) at n = 3). An ML/search over isomorphic models and factor-base subspaces with gamma/random-throw as the label tests whether any non-subfield model beats the product law. The iso_model contrast was already p = 0.028 (mean 0.990 vs 1.005) at n = 2, i.e. if anything worse.
5. **Prime-field factor-base selection**: the one within-class yield effect is factor-base points of order 2 (~2% lower). A learned or greedy selector over `prime-abscissa` subsets (avoid 2-torsion, optimise 2-sum coverage) is cheap with `csd/labels.py` (exact, vectorised, p < 2^31), priced via `native.ecbench_spec`. KN-FIND-007 (mean yield conserved) predicts only selection-level effects.
6. **Binary Semaev m = 2 Groebner cost**: regress `log_red`/`log_us` per Groebner call on (a6, subspace basis, level) using `load_binary_presentation`; needs the crypto repo's `koblitz_presentation/*.jsonl` (not in this repo). Targets learned-variable-ordering or basis-choice questions most directly.
7. **Degree-of-regularity prediction**: at toy sizes the measured degrees are constant (D_GB = Macaulay bound on all rows), so there is nothing to predict for n = 2, 3; it would require n >= 4 or unsymmetrised S_4 (not built), which is no longer cheap.
8. **Learned sieving / smoothness prediction**: no generator for smoothness of integers or polynomials exists; `binary_ghs.log2_smooth_counts` is a formula, not a sample. This would need a new generator plugin.
