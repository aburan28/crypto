Index calculus: where things stand

Can index calculus solve the elliptic-curve discrete logarithm faster than Pollard rho? Priced end to end on every curve this repository has measured, no. The one family below rho is hyperelliptic, at toy sizes. Nothing here threatens a deployed curve.

As of ledger §23 and the tool programme’s round R01 (2026-10-01). Every figure on this page is quoted from the scoreboard, the ledger this page summarises, and from the frozen files it names. When the ledger moves, this page moves in the same pull request.

The question, and how it is scored

Pollard rho is the generic method every curve is sized against; its cost is about the square root of the group order. Index calculus is a family of algebraic attacks that trade that walk for a factor base, a decomposition oracle and linear algebra. The repository asks one question of every variant it builds: with every phase counted, cold, from set-up to a checked logarithm, is it cheaper than a rho matched to the same curve?

The score is S, total operations divided by the square root of the group order, so that rho reads about 1 at every size and anything above 1 loses. Progress is a fall in the ratio to a boundary, never a fall in a constant; the reporting rule and its four classes (advance, engineering, relabelling, accounting) are what keep the ledger honest.

What is closed

  • Collision search over partial decompositions. The relation yield is bounded by a counting argument, and every lever tried left the ratio to that floor between 1.00 and 1.07. Every variant, classified.
  • The pair-table family at any size. Its optimum is 0.75 r1/6 in a unit where rho is constant, so a constant and a rising function cross once, near r = 22, and never again. At sect163k1’s size it sits about 107× the automorphism-aware walk. three regimes.
  • The “crossing” below rho. A tournament collector read 0.83× rho on an eight-cell Koblitz panel, but against a rho whose step spent most of its instructions naming Frobenius classes. Given the collector’s own orbit naming, that rho wins at every cell, by 1.19–2.88×. the control.
  • A bigger field, a bigger genus, or an auxiliary curve. Base-changing ECC2K-130 leaves its 262 automorphisms untouched and every usable subfield is either the whole field or too small; the only covers available have genus 1 or 2129; torsion and auxiliary-input routes move nothing unless the problem hands over extra powers of the secret. auxiliary inputs.
  • Cheaper oracles on their own. F4, F5, crossbred, SAT and WDSat all decide the same systems; the fastest, on every cell, is the pair table at 0–25 group additions per target where the algebraic engines need 102–106. Relation collection stays Θ(2n) with any oracle polynomial in the base. the engines.
  • Batch wins read as single-target wins. The Koblitz thread’s 1.17× of 2026-09-22 was 32 targets divided by a rho solving one; matched to batch rho it read 6.5×, and each later round moved it by engineering only. §19.

What is open

  • The tool programme. The ic tool is being made faster round by round against frozen baselines, cold and end to end with identical outputs. Baseline v0 is pinned; it reads 4.9–31.6× rho cold across six sizes, and round B3b, the index calculus on two-word binary fields, is declared. A faster tool moves S and never the ratio to the floor. the programme.
  • The extension-degree lever. At k = 4 the linear algebra grows as rho does, and the measured constant puts a plain k = 4 method at 1.9× below rho eventually, with the handover near 2151: an extrapolation on measured exponents, and far outside anything deployed. rho-parity ledger.
  • Hyperelliptic genus 4. The exponent advance holds at p ≤ 251 with the full degree-1 base; the factor-base build that dominates at cryptographic size is not modelled. hyperelliptic.
  • ECC2K-130 at scale. Any improvement meant to transfer to the challenge must first survive the m = 83 member of the same family; a result there is evidence at m = 83, and the step to m = 131 stays an extrapolation until checked. Nothing measured so far has reached that gate with a ratio below one.
  • Generic backend qualification. Two dispatches produced no auditable bundle (one cancelled at the job cap, one timed out), so no F4/F5 or SAT family qualification is established.

The rho side

The repository also runs the generic attack itself on ECC2K-130. The campaign status page shows the live distinguished-point table. The GPU client ships at 15.1 billion scalar updates a second on one RTX PRO 6000; the best tuning snapshot measured 17.95, and the 18, 20, 25 and 29 B/s targets were each priced, tried and not reached. These are practicality measurements of rho; they move no boundary. inlining, 29 B/s, occupancy.

Reading the ledger

The scoreboard is a ledger, not a summary: every experiment stays on it, negative results included, with superseded figures kept as “was” beside the current ones so an engineering step is never mistaken for an advance. Its panels are in the order the work was done. Within a panel, read three things: S (smaller is better), the ratio to the matched rho (above 1, rho wins) and the class chip, where only advance means the method did something a generic algorithm cannot.
