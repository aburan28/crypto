What was actually found

The walk hypothesis is closed. Collision search over partial decompositions cannot beat independent sampling, because the relation yield is bounded by a counting argument. Every lever tried left the ratio to that floor between 1.00 and 1.07.

Published constants are soft. The three-unknown solve lost a factor of 5.5 to ordinary linear algebra, worth fifteen bits of crossover. If the same slack exists elsewhere, quoted crossover sizes are optimistic.

The n4/9 is real. Measured 0.44 against the predicted 0.444, end to end, every run recovering the planted logarithm.

The 2/3 is exact too. Pair-only decompositions were predicted to need n2/3 residuals at k = 3; measured 0.666 over four sizes, the cleanest confirmation of a predicted exponent on this board.

One diagnosis on this board was wrong. The residuals no Macaulay degree closes were recorded as having solutions at infinity, fixable by saturation. Measured, the ideal is zero-dimensional, the affine variety carries one F_p-point where a positive- dimensional component would carry about p, and a generic change of coordinates rescued none of 28. It is the staircase border meeting the degree cut — a truncation artefact, so the fix is a border basis, not saturation.

Where the boundaries bite

The plain method never wins. Relations cost n1/3 and its linear algebra n0.68, so cost bottoms out near 200× rho around 250 and rises after. No reduction in the solve constant changes that.

The large-prime variant wins too late. Its score falls as n−1/18, so closing the measured factor of 1,987 takes roughly two hundred doublings of the group order.

One loose end. Row weight doubles across the measured range, which pushes the large-prime linear algebra to n0.56. Capping the merge level is the missing piece.

A bigger field is not a lever. Base-changing ECC2K-130 to F2131e leaves the target group and its 262 automorphisms untouched, so the reference stays 260.81 at every e. Because 131 is prime, every subfield and every Frobenius-stable subspace of that field is either of dimension ≥ 130 — a 2131 factor base, 270.19× rho at the cheapest e and rising with e — or contained in F2e, where the factor base lies inside the subgroup E(F2e) and no relation exists at any cost. Checked for e = 1…16; embedding degree > 107 closes the transfer route (experiments/ecc2k130_extension_field_boundary.json).

A bigger genus is not a lever either. A cover over F2131 is priced by that field, so the cheapest one, genus 2, costs 2131 — 270.19× rho, the same row as the bigger field. Over F2, the only proper subfield, index calculus would be cheap: genus 130 would cost 237.17, 223.64× below the reference. But because 2 is a primitive root mod 131, the GHS genus available to every curve over F2131 is 1, 2129 or 2130, and at genus 1 the transfer is exactly the zero map on the target subgroup. The window that would pay is genus 130 to between 290 and 300; the gap to the nearest available genus is 2120.77 (experiments/ecc2k130_hyperelliptic_cover_boundary.json).

Decomposing the points is not the hard part; finding the decomposition is. At the challenge parameters a target of ECC2K-130 does have factor-base decompositions — the yield law C(|F|, m)/#E holds to within 1.09× over twelve toy cells (one small-base outlier at 0.57× aside), it needs only dim V ≥ 45 at m = 3, the cofactor-4 class admits every m ≥ 2 measured on the curve itself, and certified m = 2, 3, 4 witnesses with S4 = 0 are in the artefact. Finding one is what costs: relations × targets × oracle = m · 2131 independently of the factor-base dimension, so the best implicit-base variant is 2132.58, 2+71.77× rho, and no table rescues it — at 230 through 270 entries plain BSGS on the same memory is cheaper than the best decomposition cell, and rho is cheaper than both while storing nothing. Stated as a demand on the oracle: it would have to beat exhaustive search over its own candidate set by 270.19 + log₂ m, a number that does not move with m or with l because it is the product law (experiments/ecc2k130_point_decomposition.json).

A trace-zero factor base yields twice, and that is worth exactly one bit. A base whose abscissae lie in ker Tr sits inside 2E, an index-2 subgroup, so every sum of its points stays there — and the target subgroup is inside it too. The sums are spread over #E/2, not #E, so a target is hit twice as often as C(|F|, m)/#E says. Measured exhaustively over every target of the order-r subgroup at n = 13, 17, 19: the generic base occupies 0.98–1.00 × #E and the trace-zero base 0.49–0.50 × #E, a yield factor of 1.97–2.04. The product law reads m · 2n−1 for such a base, still flat in the dimension; the table above prices a generic subspace and would move from 2132.58 to 2131.58. One bit against a gap of seventy-two; class engineering (experiments/ecc2k130_point_decomposition.json).

Accounting correction: the Frobenius collapse does not need a subspace. The classification that leaves only dimensions 0, 1, 130, 131 at n = 131 classifies invariant subspaces; GGMP's collapse asks only for π(F) = F, and since 131 is prime every abscissa outside F2 has an orbit of exactly 131, so a union of orbits is Frobenius-stable at any size. The product law becomes m · 2n/n and the bottom of the family moves from 2132.58 to 2124.99, 2+64.18× rho — paid for by materialising the base (244.5 points at m = 3, 223.4 at m = 6), because an orbit union has no low-degree membership polynomial. What 131 being prime forbids is having the collapse and the implicit base, not the collapse itself. Seven bits against a gap of sixty-four; class accounting, not a gain (experiments/ecc2k130_decomposition_targets.json).

Deciding is localising, for any detector that takes its target as input. A hypothetical decomposition oracle that answers only yes/no for the whole factor base looks far weaker than one that hands over the summands. It is not. Let R decompose; for each candidate P in the base draw a base point Q of the same cofactor class and ask the detector about R − P + Q — a class match keeps the query inside the target subgroup, so even a subgroup-only detector suffices. If P is a summand the query point is literally an m-sum of base points and the answer is yes by construction; if it is not, the point is generic and the answer is yes with probability λ. Two independent Q per candidate and the summands fall out of 2|F| whole-base queries, with no sub-base query anywhere. The group operations that build them cost k · 22l against the m · 22l the linear algebra already pays, so they are absorbed.

Measured at n = 13, 17, 19 over eight rungs, 64 built targets each, zero sub-base queries on every one. The two ways it misfires are collisions of Q with a summand or its negative — a miss at rate Θ(m/|F|) per draw, a false positive at Θ(m/|F|) + λ — and the measured rates stay inside 0.22–0.87 of those predictions across a sixteenfold range of m/|F| and of λ, with no third error term. Read off at the free-oracle optimum for m = 4 (l = 26.70, m/|F| = 2−24.70): localisation fails on one target in 220.66, losing 26.04 of the 226.70 relations needed.

So there is one free-oracle floor, not two: 256.76 at m = 4, under the 260.81 reference from m = 4 onward, monotonically, with no table. The only detector this fails against is one restricted to a fixed family of targets that refuses R − P + Q — it would have to extract witnesses by meet-in-the-middle, bottoming at 256.93 with a 253.82-entry table — and no proposed detector has that shape: a resultant-vanishing test, a trace condition, a fixed-degree Gröbner refutation and the Nagao/Riemann–Roch coefficient search are all algorithms on the target's coordinates. Two earlier revisions of this panel priced that distinction as the curve's protective margin, first at 237.68 and then at 211–224. The margin is not there; class accounting both times (experiments/ecc2k130_point_decomposition.json, experiments/ecc2k130_decomposition_targets.json).

The six pre-registered experiments have been run, and no falsifier fired. The product law is now evidence at n = 13 and n = 19 rather than arithmetic: every end-to-end rung recovered its planted logarithm, every relation was re-added in the group before entering the matrix, and the slope of log₂(total) against n is 1.037 against a falsifier of 0.95. Two rungs, not the four the design asked for — n = 11 is excluded because its 23-torsion has rank two, so no cyclic subgroup of order p exists to pose the logarithm in, and n ≥ 29 needs field tables that do not fit in memory. The slope is a two-point fit and cannot fire its own falsifier in either direction; it is reported as such.

Sweeping the factor-base dimension at n = 19 moves the yield λ by a factor of 6,600 and moves Λ = ops/2n by 2.4: the dimension is measured not to be a lever, which is the whole content of the product law. The model's predicted rise above the saturating dimension does not occur — it is an artefact of charging one relation per target — so the model is conservative there, not wrong. The Frobenius orbit collapse delivers exactly n (13.0/13, 19.0/19) with relations that reach full rank and solve, which confirms the unchecked half of the seven-bit correction above. And the yield is Poisson to 0.90–1.10 across eight cells counted exhaustively over the whole odd subgroup, once the subsets containing a point and its negative are removed; those degeneracies, not the tail, are the entire source of the raw 1.44–2.98 over-dispersion.

Two constants the law prices at one are measured optimistic. Reaching full rank took 2.0× the |F| relations the law budgets at n = 19, because decompositions harvested from one target are correlated; and large-prime pairing, whose guard does hold, produces relations that are 5–11× redundant — at m = 2 they are differences Pi − Pj and so cannot span the base at all. Exponents untouched, verdict at n = 131 untouched; class accounting (experiments/ecc2k130_decomposition_runs.json).

What would count next

The candidate this panel named has been measured. Decomposing into two base points instead of three does carry orders of magnitude: the per-residual constant falls 580×, from 0.9 million field multiplications to 1,513. It also lands far above rho, at 1,037× at 33 bits, so by this ledger's own test the fixed-degree direction is quantitatively closed at toy scale.

What is left is not a cheaper oracle. Two routes were measured and both are shut: a Gröbner solve on the descended system for fixed X₁ has a deciding degree that grows as ℓ/2, and the multilinear block structure of these systems cannot be used as a Macaulay shift restriction at all, because the only shift that preserves multilinearity is the trivial one. The open direction is a border basis in place of the fixed-degree Macaulay cut, and a merge-level cap for the large-prime linear algebra.

Nothing here threatens a deployed curve, and nothing is claimed to. The point is to locate the crossovers by measurement rather than extrapolation.
