# Near-saturated compact shared-log batch control: complete, slower than matched rho

**Decision.** Both preregistered toy-rung panels completed: n=37 R=8 and n=41 R=40 each reached full rank from the fixed 128-scalar stream, and compact and signed-Frobenius Kuhn–Struik rho independently recovered every point-only Q in all three L=8 and all three conditional L=32 blocks. The **cold compact training process plus its cold point process**, even before charging rank and scalar recovery, took longer than the same-Q cold batched rho process in every block. Thus all four fixed-stream L gates are negative on this Mac's measured wall. This is a bounded small-rung observation, not a calibrated operation crossover or an n=131 claim. Three paired blocks cannot provide a distribution-free 95% sign/permutation interval excluding parity.

The frozen protocol, target selection, stage caps and decision rule preceded outcomes in draft [PR #758](https://github.com/aburan28/crypto/pull/758). The [input specification](input_spec.json) has SHA-256 `ef2e8df8f90a6dcedbbd3d6595bfa71454d49041775980fd2ca51d0d4a67b783`. Its six 32-Q point-only files have exact L=8 prefix files, disjoint validator scalars, and no scalar labels supplied to either point producer. The source boundary is merged #747 `fc27150df3238b6863ed5618c721e7fd8b6ce403`; both Rust source hashes are in the spec and all run manifests. The measured checkout was `8c9eff1c4b876d7d6d69a6c46f6ac7a16248cafa`, using a fresh offline locked build and Python 3.9.10 / psutil 7.2.2 for 50 ms child-RSS polling. The first build attempt failed before outcomes because this sparse checkout lacked a root Cargo.lock and sandboxed DNS could not reach crates.io; its raw failure remains in `build_failure_attempt1/`. The identical-Cargo.toml lock snapshot was committed, and the successful build receipt is retained with each panel. The non-producer 32 MiB cap smoke passed and both raw receipts are committed.

## Fully charged same-Q wall decision

Here `IC lower = cold 128-query training child + cold point child`. It omits the necessary rank/base-log and point-log computation, so it is deliberately favorable to IC. `IC upper = lower + independently timed full rank/base and point-recovery **audit**`; this includes proof checks beyond an operational attack, so it is a conservative audit-inclusive bound rather than a measured optimized solve cost. Each ratio divides by the **complete cold batched rho process on the identical Q block**, with its new shared table. All times include fresh base/index setup inside each compact point child. Native IC S3/field counters and rho operation charges are reported in [analysis.json](analysis.json) but have not been calibrated to a common group-addition-equivalent `S`.

The portfolio pays one training run and one rank audit across its three blocks; its point processes still rebuild their own bases/indexes, as the current producer does. The conservative per-block comparison charges training separately to each block, exactly as preregistered. The n=37 L=8 block 0 rho process was 0.283 s rather than roughly 0.05–0.06 s for the other two blocks; the lower IC ratio still exceeds one there (2.63×), so that variation cannot reverse the fixed-stream negative gate. The maximum compact child RSS was below 35 MB on both rungs; rho was below 9 MB. RSS is not the limiting resource at these toy sizes.

The counted work explains the gap more directly than wall alone: n=37 training made 146,197 S3 calls, and each L=32 point block made 39,590–44,094; n=41 training made 18,182,518, and each L=32 point block made 5,499,904–6,317,838. Matched n=41 L=32 rho blocks used 561,441–667,413 walk steps. Those counters have different unit costs and are **not** a speedup ratio or common `S`. They show that a high-hit near-saturated base still pays expensive root construction and exhaustive partner scanning. Improving the S3 kernel or Gray-code/FES/SAT machinery could lower that per-query cost, but it does not remove the four-sum coverage/memory ceiling already established for n=131 in #747. The n=131 1 TiB materialized-root ceiling remains `8.82e-15`; a 1% necessary ceiling still requires about 1.171 EB of packed roots for that index design. A longer-sum or implicit-index architecture is a separate, gated hypothesis, not a demonstrated fix.

## Integrity and next gate

The write-once [archive ledger](evidence/SHA256SUMS) has SHA-256 `84ad0e494880e51360491276e130aea93ec16ab095ca22fe6ee53f491612f207` and covers all 26 raw child stdout/stderr files, failure-free receipts, input copies, independent validation reports, source snapshots, both panel summaries, cold build logs, RSS smoke receipts and exact source/input hashes. `verify_archive.py` independently replayed all 26/26 children with zero censors and checked the ledger; `summarize.py` recomputes the four negative fixed-stream gates from archived receipts. The PR's focused CI performs this replay without rerunning timed producers.

**Next decision:** do not spend another round only optimizing the current four-sum S3 miss loop at n=131. The separate fresh-stream exact group-law oracle in #757 addresses whether sparse-base misses were genuine support misses rather than extractor misses; this near-saturated control establishes that gaining hits on these small rungs still leaves a fully charged wall deficit. A future m=5/m=6 or implicit-index design should first demonstrate supported target mass and rank at a bounded toy rung, then charge root/index construction, failed queries, memory, rank, scalar recovery and matched batched rho in a newly frozen panel. No cost or coverage result here transfers to a descended curve or the Certicom challenge target.
