# Stronger signed-Frobenius rho reverses the compact-orbit timing lead

The preregistered four-arm, six-cell experiment completed on Linux, and all six downloaded raw artifacts passed an independent second-machine replay on macOS. Every arm solved the same point-only public Q file within its cell in each of five balanced cold blocks. Every compact run reached full rank and recovered every logarithm; every rho run recovered and verified every logarithm. The independent verifier checked all fixture `[d]G = Q` statements, 30 rank traces, base logs, compact target four-sums and recovered rho scalars. The total is 61,500 verified arm-target outputs: 5 repeats of one Q and 5 repeats of 1,024 distinct Q per n=37/41/53, for four arms. The repeated blocks measure machine variation on a fixed corpus, not target-distribution uncertainty.

The stronger reference is `rho v3 normal`: 32 independent signed-Frobenius walks sharing batched field inversions and using normal-basis rotation for orbit canonicalization. `rho v3 poly` uses the same batched-addition engine with polynomial-basis x-first canonicalization; `rho v2` is the previously corrected PCLMUL/Itoh–Tsujii reference. Each timed child includes setup, its algorithm-specific walk or index/rank work, target solving, verification and output. The rho variants use the same curve, subgroup, jump count and seed, distinguished-point rule and public Q input. Their schedules and canonical representatives can change walk paths, so this is an equal-solved-workload comparison, not a bit-identical-walk A/B. Scalar labels were never passed to the measured arms. Exact hashes, K, limits, fixture files and order are in [the freeze](FROZEN.json) and [protocol](PROTOCOL.md).

The primary paired CPU intervals are log-ratio t intervals across five same-Q blocks on each host. Every compact/normal-rho interval lies above 1. At n=37/L=1 the millisecond-scale wall timings are noisy; the CPU result is a small-instance diagnostic. Host models vary **between** cells, so no exponent or cross-n speed law is fitted from these timings. The matched batch cells used EPYC 7763 at n37, EPYC 9V45 at n41, and Xeon 6973P-C at n53; each four-arm comparison is on its own one host.

The reversal is visible without comparing different runs or hosts. On these same Q and runners, compact CPU divided by `rho v2` was **0.782** at n37/L=1,024 (95% interval 0.775–0.791), **0.391** at n41 (0.372–0.411) and **0.344** at n53 (0.339–0.346): the older comparator still lost. Against `rho v3 normal`, the paired ratios are **2.081** (2.073–2.090), **2.989** (2.877–3.116) and **3.086** (3.021–3.131), respectively. Thus the earlier batch CPU lead in [the corrected point-only panel](../compact_orbit_point_panel_20260929/RESULT.md) was comparator-engineering evidence, not a robust index-calculus timing crossover. This result does not itself prove a method-level or operation-counted loss.

The new rho's acceleration is not explained by selecting easier Q. At n41/L=1,024, median walk steps are 3,473,013 for v2, 3,862,444 for v3 poly and 3,870,901 for v3 normal; at n53 they are 18,465,354, 18,843,653 and 19,776,622. The normal backend performed **more** walk steps than v2 at both sizes yet used less CPU. At n53/L=1,024 it combined 19,776,622 active step additions into 659,215 batch inversion calls (median per block); `rho v3 normal` used 4.7567 CPU seconds versus 13.3444 for v3 poly and 42.8408 for v2. Its 2.799× CPU improvement over v3 poly includes normal-basis canonicalization and walk-path differences, not a pure one-function speed ratio. At n37/n41 batch, poly and normal walk counts differ by only 0.6%/0.2%, supporting a substantial canonicalization constant-factor effect.

The current compact implementation is already at its **rank-attempt counting floor**: exactly K rank attempts for K independent rows, with zero failed targets in all cells. The expensive work is inside those attempts and in the index. At n41/L=1,024, median compact index construction is 1.137 s, rank 0.265 s and targets 0.731 s; the entire normal rho process is 0.726 s. Index construction alone exceeds rho in every block, by 1.49–1.71×. At n53/L=1,024 the corresponding phase medians are 6.015 s, 2.706 s and 5.851 s versus 4.772 s for rho; index alone exceeds rho by 1.23–1.29× in every block. Those are wall-phase diagnostics inside the compact child, not calibrated operation units. Median compact RSS is 329/1,282 MiB at n41/n53 batch versus 40/150 MiB for normal rho. Making rank or target recovery free in the **current K/index policy** cannot make either batch process beat this reference; index construction itself must change.

**Decision and next test.** Retain the compact route as a verified full-rank, point-only method, but withdraw its batch timing lead against the strongest measured rho. The next bounded experiment should freeze a disjoint n41/n53 L=1,024 Q holdout and sweep K/base size and S3 index construction policy under the same four-arm cold harness, reporting index build, rank probes, target probes, RSS, all failures and complete process CPU. In parallel, instrument and calibrate a complete **common operation unit** for both IC and rho, including field operations, transforms, table work, rank/linear algebra, setup and verification. Only after that ledger can `S`, ratios to the generic-group floor and an attack-speed verdict be set. The n83 E₀ wider-field confidence gate and the GF(2^131) transfer remain open; this source-curve panel says nothing about degree-263 descendant PDP yield or a Certicom logarithm. A descendant factor-base comparison still needs equal useful population and n≥3 point-decomposition workloads, not just the known endomorphism-ring discriminant change.
