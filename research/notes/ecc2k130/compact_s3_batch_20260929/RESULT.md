# S3 batch inversion: full-process gain, no matched-rho crossover

The preregistered seed-622936 v2 panel completed 81 cold arms at each of n41 and n53. Every compact arm reached full rank in exactly K attempts, all 162 children exited successfully, and every arm recovered all 1,024 point-only public Q in each of nine Latin blocks. The hosted independent verifier passed 72 rank runs and 9,216 target logs **per arm and n**; there were no censored v2 cells. All four same-binary scalar A/A controls passed the frozen noise gate. Separate macOS second-host replays of both full artifacts returned byte-identical PASS receipts to Linux (SHA-256 `d23e8ca0ab09c084416e7b8d59bc3085149b90fb15e16d76410950920b7a01e6` at n41 and `948ed4ba272ec6e9c3394fdbd5355b1f655a6024538109d312ba49050147bae4` at n53). Each block repeats the same Q corpus, so paired intervals quantify run-to-run variation on a host, not target-distribution uncertainty.

The original protocol was committed before any new Q. A CI-only dead-code finding on the seed-622935 source exposed a private helper used only in tests; the candidate was annotated `#[cfg(test)]`, and the first Q were demoted to a cancelled pilot without consulting their timing ratios. The [amendment](PROTOCOL.md) and v2 source lock were committed as `54300113` before generating seed 622936; its two 1,024-point files were independently checked as `[d]G=Q` and disjoint from 5,123 earlier points per n, including the pilot. [FROZEN.json](FROZEN.json) committed those Q and separate verifier-only scalar labels at `ded946e4`, before the hosted evaluation. The source and inputs stayed unchanged for the reported run.

The n41 paired runner was Intel Xeon Platinum 8573C; n53 was AMD EPYC 7763. Compare ratios within each n, not absolute seconds across hosts. CPU seconds are user+system for the *entire* fresh child process: base construction, basis self-test, eager index, rank, linear solve, all 1,024 targets, and group checks. The boundary is the same-host normal-basis 32-walk signed-Frobenius batched rho on the same Q. A calibrated common operation unit `S=all operations/sqrt(r)` and generic-group-floor ratio remain unset; this is a host-specific engineering diagnostic, not an algorithmic ECDLP speedup.

Every W16/W64 versus same-K scalar paired CPU interval is wholly below one: batching saves about 16–26% of complete-process CPU within these cells. Every batch/rho interval is wholly above one. The observed grid minima are n41 `batch64_k255` at 1.5088 s CPU and 1.535× rho (95% CI 1.518–1.554), and n53 `batch64_k440` at 10.0206 s and 1.582× rho (1.559–1.613). Selecting the minima from this panel is exploratory. The next disjoint Q confirmation needed for a timing crossover was not triggered because neither n crosses rho.

The mechanism is bounded by the full workload. At n41 K255, W64 replaces 1,332,885 regular scalar inversions in index construction with 20,831 batch inversions while charging 3,998,655 extra Montgomery multiplications. Median index/rank/target times fall from 0.669/0.249/0.854 s for W1 to 0.538/0.217/0.718 s for W64; rho's complete CPU median is 0.979 s. At n53 K440, W64 replaces 5,130,180 regular index inversions with 80,166 batch inversions and charges 15,390,540 extra multiplications. Its median index/rank/target phases are 2.336/2.542/5.084 s, versus 3.231/3.335/6.761 s for scalar A; rho's complete CPU median is 6.335 s. The n53 W64 full process still needs about 3.686 s, or 36.8%, shaved from its measured CPU to reach parity. `exceptional_calls` records the preserved zero/equal-x fallback separately; the regular scalar/batch inversion counters exclude that fallback's polynomial-basis `gf.inv`, but cold CPU includes it. No-root returns and unconsumed prefetched candidates are charged in the traces.

The current quotient policy already meets the K+L attempt-count floor: exactly K rank attempts and one accepted witness per target. W64 preserves the same base, rank transitions, first full-point witness, recovered scalar and consumed candidate count as W1, while charging prefetched candidates. The remaining measured pressure is canonical-root lookup. At n41 K255, rank plus targets perform 3,220,797 root-table lookups and 5,073,755 slot probes for 1,279 lift attempts. At n53 K440 they perform 27,632,196 lookups and 42,477,310 slot probes for 1,464 lifts. Every root-table hit in these traces reached a valid full-point lift; the overwhelming majority of lookups miss. Peak RSS of the complete compact process is about 171 MiB at n41 K255 and 653 MiB at n53 K440. These observations nominate a **no-false-negative blocked Bloom or xor prefilter on canonical root keys** as the next bounded experiment: charge filter construction, lookup hashes, false positives, extra memory and all phases; preserve exact root/witness order; compare W64 with and without the filter on new disjoint Q and matched rho. It must cut complete CPU with a paired interval below one to count as an engineering win, and cross rho on both sizes plus disjoint confirmation before any timing-crossover claim. A stage-only miss reduction is insufficient.

**Decision.** Montgomery S3 batching is a verified complete-process engineering gain at the two tested K per n, but is a quantitative no-go for beating matched rho in these cells. This does not establish a n83, GF(2¹³¹), degree-263 descendant, or native-PDP result. The held native m≥3 gate remains separate: a full-point implicit producer and capacity admission are prerequisites before comparing crater and descended factor bases. The different degree-263 endomorphism order/discriminant is a reason to test native support there, not a reason to infer easier descended PDP from this binary S3 panel.
