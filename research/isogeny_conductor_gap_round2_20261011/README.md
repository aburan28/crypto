# Round 2: partial fibers, a fixed fiber closure, and the crater's automorphisms

**Date.** 2026-10-11.  **Status.** Measured on public synthetic prime curves.
- Six `ecbench` sessions are committed under `sessions/`, with 3,870 records,
  all verified.
- Every session was audited with twelve exact replays.

**Result class.**
- A0 is an *accounting* correction to round 1.
- E1 and E2 are *engineering* on the relation phase, plus a *boundary control*
  for the conductor gap (AGENTS.md §3).

No index-calculus cell is below its matched rho; the best is 12.8×.  No
deployed-curve claim, no key recovery.

## Findings

1. **Round 1's unexplained crater/floor set-up gap was a counting defect.**
   - The fiber oracle's subgroup closure kept adding multiples of a new
     generator after they had fallen back into the subgroup.
   - On the h = 5408 pair the floor paid 3.56× the necessary set-up
     additions, the crater 1.15× and the isomorphic model 1.00×.  On the
     h = 1568 floor it was 1.74×.
   - Under `fiber_gen=coset`, crater, floor and model pay the same set-up
     within 0.3 percent on every pair.  `S` on the h = 5408 floor falls from
     115,822 to 32,552.
   - Round 1's largest prime floor/crater `S` ratio, 3.10, becomes 1.00.
     Round 1's verdict holds and is cleaner: nothing index calculus
     measures differs across the gap.
2. **A partial fiber beats both the whole fiber and no fiber.**
   - The closed table over the subgroup `[d]E(F_p)[h]` costs:
     - 79× less than the whole fiber on the h = 1568 crater;
     - 14× less on the h = 726 floor;
     - 2.7× less on the h = 200 crater.
   - It costs 4.2× and 6.6× less than one-element tables on h = 726 and
     h = 200.  Round 1 found the fiber-closed table winning only for h ≤ 25;
     with a partial fiber it also wins at h = 200 to 1568.
   - The best subgroup holds 6 to 50 elements.  That is 2 to 12× below the
     crude model's optimum, so the location prediction failed as stated.
3. **At matched subgroup size, crater and floor cost the same.**  What
   differs is which sizes `[d]E[h]` produces.
   - The crater's ℓ-torsion is rank 2.  `[7]` kills all of the crater's
     7-part (1568 → 32) but only one factor 7 of the floor's (1568 → 224).
   - Cost at equal `|H|` agrees within 1 percent at h = 1568, within
     3 percent at h = 726, and inside the run ranges at h = 200.
   - The conductor gap changes the menu, not the price.
4. **The crater's automorphism fold pays only when the relation phase
   dominates.**
   - Folding orbits of the order-6 or order-4 group multiplies the pair
     table's set-up by about the fold squared (8.6×, 3.9×).  It divides the
     relation phase by about the same (7.5×, 3.6×).
   - On the 26-bit pairs with h = 4 and h = 18, the folded crater beats the
     floor: S 14.8 against 47.7, and 30.4 against 45.2.  That refutes the
     prediction that the transfer never pays.
   - On the other ten pairs set-up dominates, and the folded crater is 2.2
     to 8.7× worse.
   - Rho gains √3 from the same automorphisms, which this repository does
     not yet run on prime curves.  Even unfolded rho stays 12.8× cheaper.

## E1. Partial fibers

The isomorphic floor models are in `TABLES.md`.  They have the floor's
subgroup sizes, and their best cells are 279, 115 and 84.

**Against the predictions.**
- **P-E1, U-shape and win: holds.**  Cost against `|H|` is U-shaped on
  every curve.  The best partial fiber beats the whole fiber on every curve,
  by 2.3× to 79×.  Where a one-element subgroup was in the sweep (h = 726
  and 200), it beats it by 4.2× and 6.6×.
- **P-E1, location: fails as stated.**  The crude model put the optimum at
  `|H|` ≈ 33, 74 and 96.  The measured optima are 8 to 32, 6 to 33, and
  25 to 50: 2 to 12× smaller, outside the factor of 4 the protocol allowed.
  - The model assumed the relation phase falls as `1/|H|`.  It falls more
    slowly: on the h = 200 crater, doubling `|H|` from 25 to 50 cuts it
    only from 57k to 40k operations.
  - Part of each relation's cost does not scale with the table: lifting
    the target, verifying the hit, and rejecting duplicate rows.
- **The floor's h = 1568 optimum is at the edge of the sweep** (`d = 49`,
  `|H| = 32`).  Smaller floor subgroups (`d = 98`, `196`) were not swept, so
  its true optimum may be lower.  The parameters are frozen and were not
  extended after reading.
- **P-E1-gap: holds.**  At the sizes measured on both ends, crater and floor
  agree:
  - 32, 196, 392 and 1568 at h = 1568, within 1 percent;
  - 121 to 726 at h = 726, within 3 percent;
  - 8 and 50 at h = 200.  At `|H| = 8` it is 148 against 177, with run
    ranges 42–245 and 92–288.
- What the gap changes is the set of sizes `[d]E[h]` reaches.
  - The crater's 7-part, 11-part and 5-part are `Z/ℓ × Z/ℓ` or larger;
    `[ℓ]` kills them outright.
  - The floor's are cyclic, so `[ℓ]` removes one factor ℓ.
  - So at equal `d` the crater's subgroup is ℓ times smaller.  Its best `d`
    happened to land nearer the optimum on h = 1568 and 200, and further
    from it on h = 726.
  - Any subgroup size is reachable on either curve by choosing generators
    directly.  This is a property of the `[d]` parametrisation, not a
    hardness difference.

## E2. Floor → crater transfer with the automorphism fold

On every D = −3 and D = −4 pair, the crater (j = 0 or 1728) is compared with
its floor under two bases:
- `glv-orbit:size=32,group=auto` takes the crater's order-6 or order-4 group,
  and negation on the floor.
- `group=negation` is the same-column control.

Both use the coset fiber-closed MITM.  The auto ÷ negation ratio is 1.00 on
every floor and model, as it must be.

**What drives it.**  At 32 seed abscissae, the folded base on a j = 0 crater
holds 3× the points in the same 32 columns.
- The pair table holds 9× the pairs, so set-up rises by 8.6×: 37k against
  4.3k on the h = 4 pair.
- Each probe is 9× likelier to hit, so the relation phase falls by 7.5×:
  18k against 138k.
- The fold wins when the relation phase is the larger term.  That happens
  on the two 26-bit pairs with the smallest cofactors, where the subgroup is
  largest (`r` ≈ 2^22 to 2^24) and the table is small.

**Against the prediction.**
- P-E2 said the folded crater would never beat the floor.  That is
  **refuted** on two pairs, 14.8 against 47.7 and 30.4 against 45.2.
- On the other ten pairs it held, by 2.2 to 8.7×.

The protocol stopped this lead at its refutation, and no further fold
parameters were run.

**What the transfer costs.**
- Moving a floor DLP to the crater is a vertical chain of `v_ℓ(f)` Vélu
  steps of degree ℓ: at most 8 steps, of degree at most 7, here.  That is
  O(`v_ℓ(f)·ℓ`) field operations per point, below one group operation of
  the relation phase.  It is charged as zero above.
- The same automorphisms would let rho on the crater run over classes of
  size 6, gaining √3 over `rho.negation`.  This repository has no
  prime-field rho over the order-6 group, so the rho column is
  negation-only.  The folded crater's best IC/rho, 12.8×, is therefore
  flattered by up to √3.

## What this changes for the next round

- **The conductor gap is still invisible to index calculus at matched
  structure.**  It surfaces only through structure a method chooses to use:
  - which subgroups `[d]E[h]` produces (E1);
  - which automorphisms the base can fold (E2).

  In both cases the matched comparison is equal.
- **Partial fibers are the useful lever on cofactor curves.**  The next
  sweep should pick generators so that `|H|` is free of the curve's group
  structure.  It should also cover sizes 4 to 64 on every pair, which closes
  the floor h = 1568 edge.
- **An order-6 rho on prime curves** would make E2's comparison fair.
  Until then the IC/rho ratios on j = 0 craters are upper bounds on how
  close IC gets.
- **Deferred, unchanged:**
  - E4, summation polynomials symmetrised by rational 2-torsion, needs an
    `F_p` Gröbner solver.
  - E5, round 1's combined descent, needs a host that stays up for more
    than an hour.
