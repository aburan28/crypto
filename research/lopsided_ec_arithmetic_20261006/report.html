EC-arithmetic speedups from lopsided thin products: screening (2026-10-06)

Questions and method in the protocol (README.md). Rounds 1-4: thin-product interface (merged PR #1485), twelve-target survey (PR #1493), refutation audit (PR #1494), general-regime envelope (PR #1501). Status labels: [proposed] / [derived] / [verified] / [measured] (none).

Headline

No speedup: no EC kernel has the technique's shape. The method needs many wanted inner products over one shared thin middle with small-integer entries at large N. Our kernels are constant-size field formulas, sequential group operations, single inner products, or single-RHS transforms. The nearest miss dies twice over, in closed form.

Why the nearest miss misses

  • One-shot reading bound. Query time is quoted after preprocessing that already read both matrices. For |W|=1, total work is bounded below by reading the inputs -- the naive inner product. The method's power is entirely shared preprocessing over |W| >> 1; one pair amortizes nothing.
  • Entry-size bound. Entries must be N^(O(1)); ours are 256-bit field elements at every batch size. Even a hypothetical many-pair setting over F_p fails before size.

Revisit hook [proposed]

A kernel that is many small-integer inner products over one shared thin middle with known sparse wants earns a measurement protocol (matched operation counts, identical inputs/outputs), screened first against the widest table2.json row. No such kernel exists in the tree; until one is named, this screen stands.
