Item 6: the m = 4 Weil-descent degree cells, and equivariant F4

Campaign research/ic_next10_20261014/, item “### 6.” of reports/Index calculus leads next steps.md. The protocol is frozen in PROTOCOL.md, written before any main-arm data. Class: stage diagnostic. Nothing here computes an S or a rho ratio, and every n = 131 figure is an extrapolation.

Verdict

POSITIVE (levels off) at (4, 4) and at (15, 5). BLOCKED at (4, 5) and (4, 6) by the 3 GB memory cap. The kill criterion is not met.

  • The F4 solving degree does not grow with ℓ. msolve’s F4 refutes every unsatisfiable draw it finished at a maximum step degree of 4, and some at 3. That covers 56 draws:
    • m = 4: ℓ = 1, 2, 3, 4, including all four (n, ℓ) = (11, 4) draws;
    • m = 3: ℓ = 3, 4, 5, including the four committed (15, 5) draws.
  • The committed rising degrees are XL degrees, not F4 degrees. Every degree committed so far (5, 6, 6–7, 7, ≥7) is the refutation degree of bounded Macaulay (XL) linear algebra. F4 reuses its degree falls, and on the same draws it reads 4 where XL reads 5, 6, 7 and ≥7.
    • The kill criterion was written in those XL degrees: “≥8 at (4, 4) and ≥8 at (15, 5)”. On F4 it is far from met, at 4 and 4.
    • Its other half, “≤7 at both (4, 4) and (4, 5)”, holds at (4, 4). (4, 5) is BLOCKED, so the formal “reopen” condition is half-met.
  • The instrument can read degrees above 4. On random cubic systems with the same shape as the m = 4 cells, the same instrument reaches degree 6 at the (6, 2) shape and 5 at the (9, 3) shape. Those runs were cut off at 300 s; the real systems finish at degree ≤4 in 0.1 s.
  • Where the wall is. At (13, 4) (42 unknowns) and (13, 5) (46 unknowns), msolve exceeds the 3 GB address-space cap and segfaults on a failed hash-table enlargement. This happened on six attempts, at 335–839 s and peak RSS 1.6–1.8 GB, while still in degree-4 rounds. These runs are a resource limit, not evidence either way.
    • A satisfiable-draw substitute at ℓ = 5, (10, 5) with 40 unknowns, hit the same wall.
    • ℓ = 6 was not attempted. Its smallest unsatisfiable-capable n gives N ≥ 54, past the cap.
  • G2 equivariant F4: finished as one complete Macaulay pass. The pass takes block RREF over F_{2^d}, applies the inverse transform back to F₂ rows, and recovers the degree-fall rows. Its reduced row echelon form is bit-identical to the full F₂ RREF at n = 7, 11, 13, 17.
    • The equivariant pass and the full F₂ pass are timed in the same process; §3 gives the times: 44×, 58× and 73× in CPU at n = 11, 13, 17.
    • It is not wired into koblitz_groebner::reduce_system, and it is not an iterated GB computation. See “What did not get done”.

Why XL and F4 differ (mechanism, standard)

  • The XL refutation degree D_XL asks whether 1 lies in the span of {t · f_i : deg(t · f_i) ≤ D}, the original equations times monomials.
  • F4 at maximum degree D multiplies every polynomial it has found by monomials, including the degree falls. The committed first fall degree is 3 on every draw. F4 reduces those falls and multiplies them by degree-1 and degree-2 monomials, so it stays at degree 4.
  • XL instead has to reach the same products through the original equations, at degree 3 + (D − 3) plus the multiplier depth.
  • So D_F4 ≤ D_XL always. On this system the gap is 1–3 degrees, and it widens with ℓ: it is 1–2 at ℓ = 1, 2–3 at ℓ = 2, and ≥3 at ℓ = 3.
  • H2 of the protocol holds on every one of the 48 paired draws.

3. G2: equivariant F4 as a full Macaulay pass

examples/ic_next10_06_g2_full_pass.rs copies g2_wide_probe’s barrel system and block construction and adds four things.

  1. Degree-descending column order inside every block.
  2. Full RREF of each block: the trivial block over F₂ and the character blocks over F_{2^d}.
  3. The inverse transform. A reduced trivial-block row w maps to u[σ^t c] = w[c]. A character-block row maps, for each λ in an F₂-basis of F_{2^d}, to u_λ[σ^t c] = Tr(λ · w[c] · ζ^{jt}).
  4. The agreement checks. Under --control:
    • the full F₂ RREF of the symmetric matrix is computed under the same column order;
    • the RREF of the transformed rows is computed;
    • the two must be bit-identical, which is equivalent to row-space equality;
    • the degree-fall row count and the refutation bit must also agree.

  • Agreement. At n = 7, 11, 13, 17 the transformed rows’ RREF is bit-identical to the full F₂ RREF of the symmetric Macaulay matrix, under the same degree-descending order. The degree-fall row counts agree too (489, 670, 879, 1,381). Neither side refutes: the barrel system at degree 3 does not contain 1.
  • Rank identity at the new sizes. At n = 19, 23 the ranks equal g2_wide_probe’s committed full-F₂ ranks: 58,259 and 98,759.
  • Speed: 44×, 58× and 73× at n = 11, 13, 17. That is CPU time of block RREF plus inverse transform against the full F₂ RREF, in the same process.
    • It is 25–45× once the equivariant side’s orbit and row assembly is charged.
    • The full side includes building its own rows.
    • The ratio grows with n, as RESEARCH_G2_EQUIVARIANT_F4_SCOPE.md predicted.
  • n = 31 (d = 5, six character blocks). The equivariant pass finishes in 10.5 s of assembly plus 20.5 CPU s on a 275,191 × 645,566 symmetric matrix.
    • The full F₂ control would need about 21 GB dense, so it is not run.
    • So n = 31 has a measured equivariant time and no measured ratio.
    • It runs at l = 3, degree 3, not the scope note’s “dimension 16”.
  • What is transformed back differs by mode. With --control (n ≤ 17) every reduced row is transformed back. Without it (n ≥ 19) only the degree-fall rows are, which is what a solver consumes. That explains the dip at n = 19 in the figure.
  • Both sides are naive Gauss–Jordan. The control is a plain bit-packed kernel, not M4RI. The blocks use log/antilog tables. This is a same-process comparison, not a tuned-engine claim.
  • Scope. This is G2 as one complete Macaulay/XL linear-algebra pass on the Frobenius-symmetric barrel system: row space, degree-fall space and refutation bit, all recovered blockwise.
    • It is not an iterated F4 with pair selection.
    • It is not wired into reduce_system.
    • It does not apply to the random-subspace cells of §1, which have no σ symmetry. Those cells were run on msolve without G2.

Deviations from the protocol

  • Four draws at (4, 4) only. (13, 4) and (13, 5) crashed at the 3 GB cap on every attempt (two draws each, plus one rerun each with msolve -u 1). ℓ = 6 was not attempted. The protocol’s BLOCKED rule applies.
  • The FGHR arm was not run, as the protocol stated in advance.
  • The secondary no-count arm (--no-count, satisfiable draws allowed) was added after the ℓ = 5 crashes. Its single run also hit the cap. The other three jobs were stopped to free CPU.
  • Wall times come from a machine at load 15–20. Ten experiments shared four cores. Each wall time is a practicality note, and cpu_secs is recorded where the runner had it.
  • Pilots. Three msolve runs preceded PROTOCOL.md, as disclosed there. Their draws are included in the arms, not counted twice. The first arm-D run (r62-1, about 6 min, killed by hand) is kept as runs/d_pilot_r62-1_killed.log. The recorded D runs used a fixed 300 s cap.
  • The first e_sat run used an exporter built before --sat-ok reported counts. It is kept as runs/work/e_sat_oldbinary_nocount.jsonl and superseded by runs/e_sat.jsonl.

What did not get done

  • (4, 5), (4, 6), and (13, 4) at m = 4. They need more than 3 GB for msolve. Rough sizing from (11, 4)’s 1.5 GB and its matrix growth suggests 4–8 GB per draw. That is a modest host, not the 128 GB-class host the XL degree-7 runs needed.
  • G2 as a reduce_system engine and as an iterated F4. F4’s leading-term bookkeeping (a monomial order) is not σ-equivariant. The pass here produces the row space, the degree-fall space and the refutation bit blockwise, and these are what XL and Macaulay steps consume. It does not produce an F4 pair-selection loop. No src/ file was touched.
  • K, the column touches per GF(2^131) group operation. Not measured.
  • The n = 31, dimension-16 timing against macaulay_profile. Replaced by the block-only n = 31 run above, at l = 3. See G2.
