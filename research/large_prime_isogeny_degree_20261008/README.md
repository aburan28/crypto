# Large prime degree isogenies on ordinary prime-field curves: candidates and the experiments that decide between them

Status: **ideation and preregistered protocols; instruments self-tested;
no experiment run at a registered size**

Date frozen: 2026-10-08

Result class: **isogeny-computation cost study, toy and asymptotic; not an
ECDLP speedup**.  `S`, end-to-end cost and ECDLP speedup are **unset** and
stay unset whatever these experiments return.  Every claim below is about
the cost of producing one certified degree-`ℓ` edge of an ordinary
`F_p`-isogeny class for a prime `ℓ` much larger than the walker's current
cap, and about nothing else.

The experiments are in [`PROTOCOL.md`](PROTOCOL.md).  The instruments are
[`eigenvalue_orders.py`](eigenvalue_orders.py) (E1) and
[`relation_lattice.gp`](relation_lattice.gp) (E2).

## The pipeline being improved

The native walker in `src/cryptanalysis/isogeny_walk/` produces one
certified `ℓ`-edge in four stages: build `Φ_ℓ mod p` from the
`q`-expansion of `j` (`modpoly.rs`), find the roots of `Φ_ℓ(j, Y)` over
`F_p`, derive the kernel polynomial from the normalised codomain by Elkies'
power sums (`kernel.rs`), and certify the edge by division polynomials
reduced mod the kernel polynomial plus the `[g]`-closure test.  The P-256
multigraph of 2026-10-06 walked the twelve odd Elkies degrees up to 61.

Costs in `F_p` multiplications, `ℓ` the degree, `p` the field prime.  The
"current" column is read from the code; the "best known" column is from
the literature and carries the label LITERATURE-DERIVED unless marked.

| stage | current | best known | notes |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| `Φ_ℓ mod p`, once per `ℓ` | about `ℓ⁵` | `Õ(ℓ³)` | series of length `ℓ²`, schoolbook products, `ℓ²` back-substitutions; [BLS12] by CRT over volcanoes |
| `Φ_ℓ(j, Y)` per curve | `ℓ²` | `Õ(ℓ² log p)` direct [Sut13]; about `ℓ` amortised by multipoint evaluation | the walk evaluates one `Φ_ℓ` at thousands of `j` |
| roots of `Φ_ℓ(j, Y)` | `Õ(ℓ log p)` | same | `x^p mod` a degree-`(ℓ+1)` polynomial; THEOREM |
| kernel from `(a′, b′)` | about `ℓ²` | `Õ(ℓ)` [BMSS08], needs `p > 4ℓ` | power sums of `℘` at the kernel |
| edge certificate | about `ℓ²` | `Õ(ℓ log p)` | UNTESTED: Frobenius-and-`[λ]` closure in place of `[g]` closure |

The `ℓ⁵` construction was the wall.  It is why the cap sat at 61.  Nothing
downstream is worse than quadratic.  **Landed 2026-10-08 on this branch**:
the construction in `modpoly.rs` now uses a transform over `F_{p²}` for
the powers of `j` (P-256 and P-192 have `2⁹⁶ | p + 1`; `p − 1` has one
factor of two), Kronecker substitution as the fallback for other primes,
and a blockwise `O(ℓ⁴)` solve.  See [`RESULTS_E5.md`](RESULTS_E5.md).

## Candidates

Labels follow `AGENTS.md`.  "Conservative" keeps the `Φ_ℓ` route and
removes a known inefficiency.  "Representation-changing" replaces the
object the algorithm manipulates.  "Speculative" has a proof obligation
before any code.

### C1, conservative: replace the series construction of `Φ_ℓ mod p`

Compute `Φ_ℓ mod q` for many small primes `q` by enumerating `ℓ`-volcanoes
of CM curves over `F_q`, then CRT to the target `p` [BLS12].  Expected
`Õ(ℓ³)` time and `O(ℓ² log p)` space, against the current `ℓ⁵`.  A cheaper
first step is Kronecker-substitution series products in the existing
code, which removes one factor of `ℓ` without changing the algorithm.
Hypothesis H1: at the 65,536-node budget of the 2026-10-06 run the
walkable cap moves from 61 to above 1,000.

### C2, conservative: multipoint evaluation across the walk frontier

Write `Φ_ℓ(X, Y) = Σ_b P_b(X) Y^b` and evaluate all `ℓ + 1` column
polynomials at the whole breadth-first frontier with one product tree.
Per-curve cost falls from `ℓ²` to about `ℓ` once the frontier is at
least `ℓ` curves, which it is from depth 2 on.  Composes with C1; replay
contract unchanged.

### C3, conservative: BMSS kernel recovery

Replace the power-sum derivation with the differential-equation solve of
[BMSS08], valid when `p > 4ℓ`.  Kernel cost becomes `Õ(ℓ)`.  The verifier
in `kernel.rs` is untouched, so the existing 302,653 certified P-256
edges are an exact cross-check: both derivations must give the same
kernel polynomial on every edge.

### C4, conservative: opportunistic rational torsion and √élu

For each candidate `ℓ` compute, from the trace alone, the multiplicative
order `r` of the Frobenius eigenvalues `λ, p/λ` modulo `ℓ`.  When `r` is
small the kernel is generated by a point of `E(F_{p^r})`, found by one
cofactor multiplication, and the isogeny is evaluated in `Õ(√ℓ)` by
[BDLS20].  No `Φ_ℓ` is built.  Experiment E1 measures how many `ℓ` this
admits for P-256; the eigenvalue heuristic predicts about `R²/ℓ` of the
split primes have `r ≤ R`.

### C5, representation-changing: the `ℓ`-isogenous curve through the class group

For a horizontal split `ℓ` the two `ℓ`-neighbours are `𝔩·E` and `𝔩̄·E`
for the prime ideal `𝔩` over `ℓ` in the Frobenius order `O`.  Compute
`Cl(O)` once, express `𝔩` as a short product of ideals of small norm
through the relation lattice, and walk those small degrees with the
existing certified code.  [BKV19] did the analogous computation for a
512-bit discriminant; P-256's `D_π` is 258 bits.  Hypothesis H5: a
degree-`ℓ` edge for any split `ℓ` up to `2⁶⁴` costs a few hundred
small-degree steps, independent of `ℓ`.

Literature check, 2026-10-08, after freezing: the relation-lattice form
of this candidate is Jao–Soukharev's subexponential method [JS10], and the
action of an invertible ideal of polynomial norm is now computable in
polynomial time by Page–Robert's Clapoti(s) [PR23] and its refinements
(PEGASIS, KLaPoTi) through higher-dimensional isogenies.  So the
polynomial-time version of C5 exists in the literature; what E2 still
measures is the concrete relation norm against this walker's `Φ_ℓ` route,
and what remains open for this class is only engineering.  Vertical steps,
where `ℓ` divides the conductor, are not covered by [PR23], which acts
through invertible ideals only.

What this does and does not give.  It gives the codomain `j′` and an
`F_p`-isomorphism certificate between the codomain of the small-degree
walk and the claimed `ℓ`-neighbour.  It does not give the degree-`ℓ`
kernel polynomial, because the composite walk has degree `∏ q_i^{e_i}`,
not `ℓ`.  So it certifies edge existence, not the explicit isogeny.  It
also reframes the stalled `2³²`-population goal of
`research/p256_isogeny_multigraph_64k_20261006/` as navigation in
`Cl(O)`, which is the actual geometry of that class.  Experiment E2
measures the relation norms and checks the route against the walker's
certified edges for `ℓ ≤ 61`.

### C6, representation-changing: Atkin or Weber level-`ℓ` polynomials

Degree `ℓ + 1` in the auxiliary modular function but about `ℓ/12` in `j`,
so roughly one-twelfth the coefficients of `Φ_ℓ` and much lower height
[Sut13].  Needs a conversion from the Atkin root back to `j′`.  Composes
with C1 and C2.  No experiment is registered here; it is a size
reduction whose benefit is read off the C1 timing table.

### C7, speculative: dimension-2 embedding of an ordinary `ℓ`-isogeny

In the supersingular setting, large-prime-degree isogenies are now
computed as components of `(2ᵉ, 2ᵉ)`-isogenies through Kani's lemma when
`ℓ + d = 2ᵉ` and an auxiliary `d`-isogeny is known.  For ordinary `E/F_p`
with `End(E) = O` known from C5, the auxiliary isogeny is an element of
`O` of norm `d`.  For horizontal `ℓ` this is exactly what
[PR23] and PEGASIS do, so C7 is not new there and is withdrawn as a
candidate for horizontal steps.  What survives is the vertical case, `ℓ |
f_π`, where the kernel is not cut out by any invertible ideal.  A scoped
obstruction, THEOREM with a two-line proof in the JMV note of 2026-10-08:
every endomorphism of a floor curve that kills the ascending `ℓ`-kernel
has degree divisible by `ℓ²`, so a QFESTA-style extraction from an
endomorphism of norm `ℓ · (2ᵉ − ℓ)` cannot exist on the floor.  Any
dimension-2 route to a vertical step must therefore start from torsion
images, not from an endomorphism.  UNTESTED beyond that obstruction; no
code until a torsion-image construction is written out.

## Assumptions and model

- Ordinary curves over prime fields; `ℓ` split in `Z[π]`; `ℓ ∤ f_π`;
  `p > 4ℓ`.
- The cost unit is `F_p` multiplications, as in the ecbench harness.
  Extension-field work in C4 is charged at `r²` base multiplications per
  `F_{p^r}` multiplication.
- The baseline is the native walker at `ℓ = 61` and its 302,653-edge
  replay, which is the correctness oracle for every candidate.
- Class invariants of registered P-256, computed 2026-10-08 with PARI and
  consistent with the walker's `ClassInfo`: `t =
  89188191154553853111372247798585809583`; `D_π = t² − 4p` is 258 bits,
  `D_π ≡ 1 (mod 4)`, and `−D_π = 3 · 5 · q₁ · q₂ · q₃` with
  `q₁ = 456597257999`, `q₂ = 1428624589419343516204097` and
  `q₃ = 46523541035814968339936406074986559003387`, three distinct primes
  (`factorint`, about one minute; FactorDB holds the same factorisation
  of `|D_π|` under id `1100000008921609430`, status `FF`, checked
  2026-10-08).  So `D_π` is fundamental, `Z[π]` is
  the maximal order, and `End(E) = Z[π]` for every curve in the class.
  The walker's trial division stops below `q₁` and records the cofactor
  as `composite_unfactored`; this factorisation closes that.  Degrees 3
  and 5 are ramified, which is why the walker records one rational
  neighbour at each.

## Reading guide for the results

Each experiment in the protocol has a registered decision rule.  The
combined reading is one table: for each `ℓ` in the sweep, which of C1, C4
and C5 produces a certified edge at least cost, and at which `ℓ` the
representation changes.  If C5's relation norms are small, `Φ_ℓ` is only
needed when the kernel polynomial itself is wanted, and the walk cap
question dissolves.  If they are large, C1 carries the walk to about
`10³` and C4 carries the rare small-order `ℓ` beyond that.
