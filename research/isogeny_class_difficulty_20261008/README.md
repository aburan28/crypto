# ECDLP difficulty across an isogeny class, large prime degrees included, and a methodology for finding weak curves in a class

Status: **preregistered protocols; nothing run.**  Frozen 2026-10-08.
Result class: **boundary and screening study; not an ECDLP speedup.**
`S`, end-to-end cost and speedup are **unset** for every protocol here and
stay unset whatever they return.

Companion threads: [`../large_prime_isogeny_degree_20261008/`](../large_prime_isogeny_degree_20261008/)
prices *computing* one certified degree-`ℓ` edge for large prime `ℓ`;
[`../isogeny_conductor_gap_20261007/`](../isogeny_conductor_gap_20261007/)
and the depth-1 census (#1556) look for Joux–Vitse-weak neighbours;
[`../isogeny_volcano_ic_20260924/`](../isogeny_volcano_ic_20260924/) is the
volcano/IC campaign.  This directory asks the question those leave open:
**does ECDLP difficulty differ between a curve and its large-prime-degree
isogenous neighbours, and how would one find, in a class, the curve on
which the DLP is cheapest to solve?**

## The theorem that frames every experiment

Let `φ: E → E′` be a separable isogeny of prime degree `ℓ` defined over
`F_q`, and let `r` be the prime subgroup order with `ℓ ≠ r`.  Then `φ`
restricted to `E[r](F_q)` is a group isomorphism onto `E′[r](F_q)`, and for
`Q = [d]P`, `φ(Q) = [d]φ(P)`.  So:

    DLP_E(P, Q)  ≤  cost(φ(P)) + cost(φ(Q)) + DLP_{E′}(φ(P), φ(Q)),

and symmetrically through the dual.  **Within an isogeny class, difficulty
is invariant up to transport cost.**  Every generic-group attack (rho,
BSGS, kangaroo) sees only `r` and the automorphism group, so it cannot
distinguish isogenous curves at all.  Only three things can vary inside a
class:

1. **Transport cost** `T(E → E′)`: finding the route plus evaluating it on
   two points.  Horizontal degree-`ℓ` edges correspond to ideal classes of
   the Frobenius order, and under GRH the class group is generated by
   primes of norm below the Bach bound, so a large-`ℓ` neighbour is also
   reachable by small-`ℓ` steps: large `ℓ` changes *cost*, never *reach*.
   Vertical edges (`ℓ | f_π`) change the endomorphism ring and are the one
   place a large-`ℓ` edge reaches something small steps do not, when `ℓ`
   is a large prime dividing the conductor.
2. **Automorphisms**: `|Aut(E)| ∈ {2, 4, 6}`; the extra automorphisms at
   `j ∈ {0, 1728}` give rho its `√|Aut|/√2` factor.  These `j` are class
   members only when `D_π ∈ {−3, −4}` times a square, i.e. almost never.
3. **Model-dependent non-generic structure**: anything an index-calculus,
   descent or cover method reads off the *equation* rather than the
   group — the GHS magic number `m(b)` (binary, composite degree; not an
   isogeny invariant, which is exactly why Galbraith–Hess–Smart walk the
   class to find a small-`m` neighbour), cover existence for `E/F_{p^n}`,
   subfield-definedness of coefficients, and the relation yield of a fixed
   factor-base family.

Class invariants that **cannot** vary and are therefore not experiments:
`#E`, `r`, the trace, the embedding degree (a function of `r` and `q`),
anomaly, and `D_π`.  A protocol that "finds" variation in these has a bug.

So the measurable questions are exactly these, and each protocol below
takes one:

## The weak-curve methodology (what the protocols feed)

A curve is **weak in its class** when some non-generic method solves the
DLP on it, transport included, for less than rho on the original.
Define, for a target `E` with prime subgroup order `r` and a class
`C(E)`:

    W(E) = min over E′ ∈ C(E) reachable within budget of
           [ T(E → E′) + N(E′) ] / R(E),

with `R(E)` the matched strong-rho cost (ecbench unit, negation map and
any `Aut` folding applied), `N(E′)` the cheapest *measured* non-generic
whole-pipeline cost on `E′` (never a stage), and `T` the route cost from
I-2.  `W < 1` is a weak class; `W ≥ 1` is not, whatever any single stage
looked like.  The procedure:

1. **Enumerate or sample the class.**  Small-`ℓ` breadth-first walk with
   the native walker (`isogeny_walk walk`, certified `IW1` edges) for prime
   fields; the `Φ_ℓ mod 2` walk (`binary_isogeny`) for binary fields.
   Large-`ℓ` neighbours enter through the rational-torsion window (C4 of
   the companion thread) or the class-group route (C5); vertical large-`ℓ`
   edges, when `ℓ | f_π`, through the kernel from `E[ℓ](F_{q^k})`.
2. **Screen every node** with the cheap model-dependent invariants of I-3:
   `m(b)` for every factorisation of the extension degree (binary), cover
   existence and genus (`curve_cover_check`), `j` in a proper subfield,
   coefficients in a proper subfield, `|Aut|`, and the typed link record of
   [`../../docs/curves/ic/curve-links/README.md`](../../docs/curves/ic/curve-links/README.md).
   A screen is a *lead*, never a result: it predicts a small `N(E′)`.
3. **Price the route** `T(E → E′)` with I-2's table, including finding it.
4. **Run the whole non-generic pipeline** on each screened `E′` with a
   hidden scalar, verify `Q = [d]P` on `E` after transport back, and
   charge every phase (factor base, failed searches, relations, linear
   algebra, recovery, the two transports).
5. **Run matched strong rho on `E`** under the same `ecbench` spec, same
   `r`, same host class, A/A interval first.
6. **Controls before any claim**: coordinate change and isomorphic model of
   `E′`; a second field representation (binary) or a second irreducible
   (prime extension); an independent implementation of the oracle where
   one exists (`enumerate`, `groebner`, `sat`, `wdsat`, `smt`); fresh
   classes held out for confirmation; Benjamini–Hochberg over every screen
   feature tested in the same round; timeouts and failures kept and
   reported.
7. **Classify** by `AGENTS.md` §3 and the brief's five labels: known
   weakness, implementation effect, statistical artifact, reproducible
   unexplained anomaly, supported new attack hypothesis.  Only the first
   and last change a ledger.

Positive controls the methodology must recover before it is trusted:

- Binary composite degree: the Galbraith–Hess–Smart isogeny extension of
  GHS.  Over `F_{2^{n}}` with composite `n`, a random class contains
  curves whose `m(b)` differs; the class minimum is the weak curve, and
  the fraction of weak classes is in the literature (Menezes–Teske for
  `n = 5·31`, `7·23`, …).  I-3 must reproduce the distribution.
- Binary prime degree: `ECC2K-130`'s field has no proper intermediate
  subfield; `m(b) = n` for every ordinary curve in every class, so I-3
  must return a constant and `W ≥ 1` from this route.  §8b of `AGENTS.md`
  governs how that is stated.
- Prime field, `F_p`: no cover and no descent exist; the only model
  structure is `Aut`.  I-4 is expected to be flat and I-5 to show exactly
  the `√|Aut|/√2` factor at `j ∈ {0, 1728}` and nothing else.  A prime-field
  class that is not flat is the finding this whole directory exists to
  catch, and it is held to every control in step 6.

## Frozen inputs shared by the protocols

- Prime-field ladder: `p ≈ 2^{20}, 2^{24}, 2^{28}, 2^{32}` with
  prime-order or near-prime-order random ordinary curves (cofactor
  `≤ 4`), three classes per size, CM-constructed `D = −3` and `D = −4`
  classes added at `2^{20}` and `2^{24}` for I-5.  Curves are registered by
  ICV1 slug in the follow-on PR that first runs them.
- Binary ladder: the registered `icv1-f2m13-t181-515ee569`,
  `icv1-f2m19-t797-b6cf2467`, `icv1-f2m23-t5197-69e76b73` (prime degree,
  negative control) and composite-degree `n ∈ {12, 15, 21}` classes
  registered in the follow-on PR (positive control).
- Degrees: small `ℓ ≤ 61` (walker cap); large `ℓ` from the sweep of the
  companion thread's E1 window and, where it lands, C5's class-group route;
  vertical `ℓ` wherever `f_π` has a prime factor above 61 at toy size.
- Seeds: one per protocol, named in each file; hidden scalars drawn by the
  harness and never written before verification.
- Unit: `ecbench` counted group operations with the field-operation split;
  wall time only as an isolated-host practicality note.

## Stop condition for the directory

Each protocol is bounded on its own.  The directory closes when I-1, I-2
and I-5 have confirmed the invariance picture at toy size and I-3 and I-4
have either reproduced the known positive controls with flat prime-field
results, or produced a prime-field anomaly that survived step 6, in which
case it gets its own protocol at the next size.  No extrapolation from
`p ≤ 2^{32}` or `n ≤ 23` to P-256 or ECC2K-130 is admissible in this
directory.
