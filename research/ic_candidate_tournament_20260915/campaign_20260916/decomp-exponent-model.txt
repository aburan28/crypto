====================================================================================================
Part A. Generic j-table decomposition: table of all j-sums of an F-point base, (m-j) summands
looped per trial, one relation per column.  Cost F^j + r/F^(j-1)  =>  r^(j/(2j-1)).
Holds for any m >= j; the summand count only moves constants.  Rho is r^(1/2).
====================================================================================================
  j          table  exponent    ratio IC/rho grows as  memory at optimum
  1      base only    1.0000                 r^0.5000  r^1.000 entries
  2     pair table    0.6667                 r^0.1667  r^0.667 entries
  3   triple table    0.6000                 r^0.1000  r^0.600 entries
  4    4-sum table    0.5714                 r^0.0714  r^0.571 entries
  6    6-sum table    0.5455                 r^0.0455  r^0.545 entries
  j -> inf: exponent -> 1/2 from above, never below.  Every such method is generic (it uses the
  group law, equality of encodings and the Frobenius, which is [lambda] and costs O(log r) group
  operations to simulate), so Shoup's Omega(sqrt r) generic lower bound (Eurocrypt 1997) forbids
  an exponent below 1/2 for the whole family, whatever the table, fold, walk or summand count.
  Measured two-point rate of the pair arm vs the unmatched rho (RESULTS.md round 0021): r^0.153;
  model r^(1/6) = r^0.1667.

====================================================================================================
Part B. Algebraic subspace decomposition, free-form solve cost.
Base: points with x in an l-dim F_2-subspace V (|F| ~ 2^l).  H1: a target decomposes with
probability min(1, 2^(m l)/(m! 2^n)) (the repo's yield law, confirmed to 1.09x on 12 toy cells).
Relations needed ~ |F|; linear algebra ~ |F|^2.  Per-trial solve cost T = 2^(c n).
With x = l/n the log-cost/n is max(1 - (m-1)x + c, 2x) for x <= 1/m, so:
   E(c, m) = 2(1+c)/(m+1)      if c <= 1/m      (balanced optimum x = (1+c)/(m+1))
   E(c, m) = 1/m + c           if c >  1/m      (saturated optimum x = 1/m)
E < 1/2 (an exponent below rho) iff c < c*(m):
====================================================================================================
  m    c*(m)   E(0,m)   gamma* chained S3 (N=(m-1)n at x=1/m)   gamma* direct S_(m+1) (N=n)
  3   0.0000   0.5000                                 0.0000                       0.0000
  4   0.2500   0.4000                                 0.0833                       0.2500
  5   0.3000   0.3333                                 0.0750                       0.3000
  6   0.3333   0.2857                                 0.0667                       0.3333
  7   0.3571   0.2500                                 0.0595                       0.3571

  9   0.3889   0.2000                                 0.0486                       0.3889
 10   0.4000   0.1818                                 0.0444                       0.4000
  m = 3 cannot go below 1/2 even with a free oracle (E(0,3) = 1/2): it ties rho at best.
  gamma* is the largest per-unknown exponent (T = 2^(gamma N)) a solver may have on the
  descended system and still beat rho asymptotically.  Exhaustive search is gamma = 1; the
  generic j-table of Part A is what 'solving' by the group law costs.  So the algebraic route
  needs the descended systems to be far easier than generic Boolean systems of their size.

Part C. Algebraic subspace decomposition priced under four solve laws, fold = n (stable V),
chained S3 unknowns N = m l + (m-2) n, Macaulay cost T = (monomials of degree <= D)^omega.
  L_free : T = 1                         (the repo's free-oracle floor)
  L_const: D = m^2 - m + 1, omega = 2    (Kousidis-Wiemers first-fall bound used AS a solving
                                          degree: the first-fall-degree assumption, disputed)
  L_sem  : D = 4 for every m, omega = 2 (Semaev ePrint 2015/310 Assumption 1 on chained S3;
                                          the assumption Huang-Kosters-Yeo turn into a reductio)
  L_meas : D = 3.5 + l/2,  omega = 2     (fit to this repo's m = 3 refutation degrees 5,6,6,7,7
                                          over l = 3..7; carried to m >= 4 unmeasured, which is
                                          optimistic if m = 4 degrees are higher)
Direct-S_(m+1) variant (N = m l, same D laws) shown as 'dir' for L_const: the optimistic reading.

Reading Part C: negative = cheaper than matched rho.  'slope' is the empirical exponent of the
model's IC cost per unit n at large n (rho's is 0.5).  L_free is a floor nothing reaches;
L_meas is the only law with measured support, and it is m = 3 support only.  Finite-n slopes
need not equal the asymptote E(0, m) = 2/(m+1) of the constant-degree laws.
